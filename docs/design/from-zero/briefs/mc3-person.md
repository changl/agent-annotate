# MC3. Judge the four machine designs: what he lives through

Your full brief is `docs/design/from-zero/briefs/mc-machine-critic.md`. Read it first and follow it exactly.

Your file: `docs/design/from-zero/research/machine-critique-person.md`.

Your lens: **what the person lives through.** The machine exists only to spend less of his attention for the same or better results. Judge each design from his side alone.

1. Replay all 14 events of the test week through each machine. For each event: what reaches him, when, in what form, and what he must do. Count, for the week, using the list at the end of the scenario file: places to look, interruptions, things held in his head between sittings, words read, words typed, decisions (and how many only he could make), minutes.
2. Does each machine really let an agreement be accepted in about a minute? What must have happened behind the screen for that to be true, and how often will it fail, so that he must type a paragraph instead?
3. Find where each machine quietly pushes work back onto him: keeping rules tidy, setting how much he trusts what, reading proofs, approving defaults, sorting out conflicts, explaining what he meant again.
4. Live through a bad day in each: three stalls, a flood of questions, one thing that cannot wait, and one result that turns out wrong.
5. Live through day 30 as well as day 1. What has got lighter? What has got heavier (more rules, more goals, more history)?
6. Come back after five days away in each. What does he face?
7. Use the questions data in `research/d2-questions.md`: his answers today carry about 38,900 typed words, a third of answers have typed words, and the largest kinds of question are how the work should behave, accepting a screen, and correcting an instruction. For each kind, say what each machine would do differently, and how much of that typing it would plausibly remove. Be sceptical.

End your lens section with: which design he would thank you for after a month, which he would abandon and why, and the five things he must never be asked to do.
