# The letter

**The idea in one sentence.** One short letter a day, at the hour he chose, already answered in pencil wherever he has allowed it; he inks, changes or ignores the pencil, marks the enclosed thing itself where it is wrong, and everything else stays silent behind a glance that says how fresh it is, or that it cannot see.

Open `index.html`. "next" in the striped bar steps through the week; "notes" says what he does not see. Under 1040 px wide it is his phone, above that his desk. [1] to [7] mark what the outside evidence changed.

## The ten principles

1. **Attention.** The letter is cut to his ten minutes, not to the work. Everything arrives prepared and checked. Minutes spent on the counterpart's miss are counted too.
2. **Reacting.** He reacts to a say-back, then a sketch, then the thing. He can still change a line in words; a sketch does not settle everything.
3. **Agreement first.** The say-back states what "go" allows. "Change what I asked" reopens it when facts change.
4. **Decide once.** A send-back asks "here only, or always?". His rules are acted on; the counterpart's guesses only shape what it would say, and each can be struck.
5. **Silence.** Nothing is pushed but the buzz. Silence carries its age, and says "I cannot see" rather than "nothing needs you" [1].
6. **Nothing waits silently.** Every item says what happens if he leaves it: inside a permission it goes ahead at a stated time; outside, it waits, then parks, and says so [2].
7. **The thing, checked.** The enclosure is the thing. The evidence, what was not checked and the checker's own words are one touch away [6].
8. **One doorway.** One letter in, one button to speak. And two ways around it, so the doorway is never the only witness [6].
9. **Trust by kind.** One acceptance earns nothing. An offer comes after several that went well and stayed well, keeps one in five at random, ends on a miss or a new kind, and is never made for what cannot be undone [3].
10. **No more than he can finish.** The limit is his minutes and the age of what waits; new work starts only as fast as he clears it [4].

Served worst: **7, for anything that moves.** A running app, or a behaviour over time, cannot be enclosed. The letter shows stills and hands him to the real thing. Next worst is 2: a fixed hour puts up to a day between his reaction and the next thing to react to.

## The week, counted from the prototype

Its self-check (`index.html#check`) presses the real buttons and counts. Phone first, desk in brackets.

- **Places he must look:** 1, the letter, on a screen he already looks at. The glance and "behind" are optional.
- **Interruptions:** 1, the sign-in buzz. The letter arrives without a sound, and so does the blind hour.
- **Held in his head between sittings:** 0. Whatever he says, parks or sends back is confirmed as held, with its return.
- **Words read:** about 2,050 (1,600 before the evidence: permissions, what was not checked and the checker's words cost reading). Letters about 1,390; the things 218; the weekly page about 275 with two lines opened; the buzz 61; glances and the heard-back about 100. Curiosity adds about 140.
- **Words typed:** 0. Spoken: 20 (an 11-word goal and a 9-word correction).
- **Taps:** 40 (35). Letters 5, 13, 7, 7 (4, 12, 6, 5); 1 hold to speak; 1 sign-in; 4 on the weekly page; 2 behind.
- **Decisions:** 21. Only he could make 15. The other 6 were results shown for want of earned trust, and one choice that would have gone ahead inside a permission. Only one of those kinds stops being shown after this week.
- **Minutes:** about 37. Letters 8, 9, 6, 9; the buzz 1; saying it under 1; the weekly page 3. Four of them went on the counterpart's miss.

## Left out, and what I would cut next

Left out: any app, home screen, inbox or history in front of him; a list of questions; projects as places; status; a settings screen; a send button; a count limit; every notification but the buzz. **Cut next:** the folding, if letters stay under four things; then the read-aloud, if he never uses it.

## Three weakest points

1. **Moving things do not fit in a letter.** For behaviour, speed or feel he must leave it, and pointing is lost on the way out.
2. **A permission is only as narrow as its words.** "Order, layout and wording on the billing pages" is stretched or not by the counterpart, and a wrong stretch looks like a rightful default. "Yes to all three" still inks three answers in one press.
3. **It lives on surfaces it does not own.** "I cannot see" depends on the phone running the 15-minute count. If the phone will not refresh the widget, a stale "Nothing needs you" stays up: the exact failure it exists to prevent.

## Where I left the premise

- **The enclosure is a real surface**, not part of a letter: full size, its own colours, markable, with its evidence. Principle 7 demands it.
- **The letter is alive.** It is rewritten in place, folds, answers his marks and adds a P.S. A paper letter would pile up (principles 1 and 10).
- **One push exists**, the buzz, against "nothing pushed", because of principle 6.
- **The glance opens into a standing screen**: the goals, the permissions and the way out. Reached only by choice, but it is a small app (principles 5 and 9).
- I added to the scenario one small goal (reworded error messages, for event 10), one question (learners who turned texts off) and one moment (the watcher goes dark).
