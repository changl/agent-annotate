# The letter

**The idea in one sentence.** One short letter a day, at the hour he chose, already answered in pencil by a counterpart that has decided everything it can; he inks, changes or ignores the pencil, marks the enclosed thing itself where it is wrong, and everything else stays silent behind a glance that tells the truth.

Open `index.html`. "next" in the striped bar steps through the week; "notes" says what he does not see. Under 1040 px wide it is his phone, above that his desk.

## What exists

| Thing | What it is for | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| The letter | One sheet, never a pile. It is rewritten in place, so three days away is still one letter. Fitted to him (under ten minutes, seven things at most) and it ends: "That is all." One thing lies open, the rest fold to a line each; a mark folds it and opens the next. | Intent, sight |
| The say-back | An item in the letter: you said, I heard, done means, I'll decide, only you, not doing, next. Touch any line to change it. | Intent |
| Pencil | A dashed circle marked "I'd say": the counterpart's own answer, only ever on the side that can be undone, with the time it goes ahead by itself. His blue ink confirms or overrules it. | Trust |
| The enclosure | The result itself (a file, an email, a sketch, three screens) in its own colours: beside the letter on the desk, full screen on the phone. Touch a line to cut it or say what is wrong; touch a spot on a picture to pin it; or one remark on the whole. Then asked once: here only, or always? | Sight, intent |
| The glance | A lock-screen widget and a corner of the desk screen. "Nothing needs you", and when that was last checked by something that does none of the work. Opens into where every goal stands. | Sight |
| Hold and say | One button on the glance. A goal in his words, heard back at once, said back in the next letter, or now if he asks. | Intent |
| The letter read aloud | The same letter by voice, answered by voice. What needs his eyes is kept, not forced. | Sight |
| The buzz | The only sound. Allowed only when all three hold: only he can do it, it cannot wait for the next letter, missing it loses something named. Once. Its cause and the fix go on the weekly page. | Trust |
| The weekly page | The back of the letter: goals done, how often each reached him against plan, his minutes, what was decided for him, rules learned, send-backs, misses, buzzes, stalls, what may now go ahead unshown. | Trust |
| Behind the letter | Who is working where, and what one piece of work has cost. Reached only by asking. | Sight |
| Behind all of it | The record; a checker who is never the maker; a watcher that does none of the work. | Trust |

## The ten principles

1. **Attention.** The letter is cut to his ten minutes, not to the work. Everything arrives prepared, checked, and with an answer pencilled in.
2. **Reacting.** He never writes. He reacts to a say-back, then a sketch, then the thing.
3. **Agreement first.** The say-back is an item in the letter. Nothing starts before "Go".
4. **Decide once.** Every send-back asks "here only, or always?". The weekly page lists the rules; he can strike one.
5. **Silence.** Nothing is pushed but the buzz. The glance says when it was last checked, and by what.
6. **Nothing waits silently.** Every item says what happens if he leaves it, and when. Pencil goes ahead; what needs his eyes keeps, and says so.
7. **The thing, checked.** The enclosure is the thing. "All 6 done lines pass, checked by a helper who did not build it."
8. **One doorway.** One letter in, one button to speak. No projects, helpers or machines on it.
9. **Trust by kind.** The first of a kind is shown. Three clean acceptances bring a P.S. offering "do and report". A miss takes it back.
10. **No more than he can finish.** Seven things at most; nothing new is started while his letter waits.

Served worst: **7, for anything that moves.** A running app, or a behaviour over time, cannot be enclosed. The letter shows stills and hands him to the real thing. Next worst is 2: a fixed hour puts up to a day between his reaction and the next thing to react to.

## The week, counted from the prototype

Its self-check (`index.html#check`) presses the real buttons and counts. Phone first, desk in brackets.

- **Places he must look:** 1, the letter, on a screen he already looks at. The glance and "behind" are optional.
- **Interruptions:** 1, the sign-in buzz. The letter arrives without a sound.
- **Held in his head between sittings:** 0. Whatever he says, parks or sends back is confirmed as held, with its return.
- **Words read:** about 1,600. Four letters 1,073; the things themselves 218; the weekly page about 180 with two lines opened; the buzz 61; glances and the heard-back about 85. Curiosity adds about 120.
- **Words typed:** 0. Spoken: 20 (an 11-word goal and a 9-word correction).
- **Taps:** 38 (33). Letters 5, 13, 7, 6 (4, 12, 6, 4); 1 hold to speak; 1 sign-in; 3 on the weekly page; 2 behind.
- **Decisions:** 19. Only he could make 12. The other 7 were pencil he could have left alone, or first-of-a-kind results; three of those kinds stop being shown after this week.
- **Minutes:** about 37. Letters 8, 9, 6, 9; the buzz 1; saying it under 1; the weekly page 3.

## Left out, and what I would cut next

Left out: any app, home screen, inbox or history in front of him; a list of questions; projects as places; status; a settings screen (he says "send it at 7"); a send button (each mark takes effect as he makes it); every notification but the buzz.

Cut next: the list of where everything stands (one line at the foot of the letter could carry it); then the folding, if letters stay under four things.

## Three weakest points

1. **Moving things do not fit in a letter.** For behaviour, speed or feel he must leave it, and pointing is lost on the way out.
2. **The pencil invites a yes without reading.** One press inks three questions of policy. The design counts kept pencil as trust earned, and cannot tell agreement from haste.
3. **It lives on surfaces it does not own.** A phone may refresh a widget late, and the buzz sits among every other app's. If the glance is ever stale, silence has lied once, and he goes back to checking.

## Where I left the premise

- **The enclosure is a real surface**, not part of a letter: full size, its own colours, markable. Principle 7 demands it.
- **The letter is alive.** It is rewritten in place, folds, answers his marks and adds a P.S. A paper letter would pile up (principles 1 and 10).
- **One push exists**, the buzz, against "nothing pushed", because of principle 6.
- **The glance opens into a list of goals**: a small standing screen, reached only by choice (principle 5).
- I added one small goal to the scenario (reworded sign-up error messages) so that event 10's third acceptance has something to happen on.
