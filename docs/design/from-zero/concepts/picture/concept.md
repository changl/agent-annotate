# One true picture

**The idea.** One page, set like a book's table of contents: everything he has asked for, in his words, each in a fixed place and showing only when he will next see it. The few things that are with him are marked in red ink. A sentence at the top says what is with him now, or when the page will next have anything for him, so looking early tells him nothing new.

## Components

| Component | What it is for | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| The page | Every goal in his words, under the thing it belongs to (Driving app, Billing tool…), in a place that never moves. Each line shows only a promise: a day ("Thu", "sketch Thu"), "parked: after sign-up", or "done". | Sight |
| The top sentence | "Three things are with you, about 8 minutes", or "Nothing needs you. Next: the welcome email sketch, **at 17:00 today**." "Same as when you looked at 12:41" when nothing changed. After an absence, two lines on what changed. | Sight; works against checking |
| Red ink | The only colour on the page. It marks what is with him, on its own line ("what I heard", "ready to see", "your choice", "can't wait"). **Begin** goes through them in the order where delay costs most. | Sight |
| The thing, opened where it sits | Phone: it opens under its line. Desk: it opens on the facing page, with its line underlined. Kinds: what I heard (the agreement), a sketch, the result, a choice. Each choice says what I'd pick and what happens if he says nothing. | Intent; trust |
| Pointing | Every line of an agreement, paragraph of a sketch or row of a file can be tapped. He marks it, picks or says a reason ("too stiff"), then answers once: *here only, or always?* | Intent; decide once |
| Told-once notes | A dot and one line under the line they are about: a promise moved (old day struck through), a question withdrawn, back from parked. They are gone the next time he looks. | Sight |
| Say what you want | One field and a mic. No project, no form. Where the new line lands is how I say back what I understood ("It's about the Driving app, so it sits here"). | Intent |
| Not now, with a return | Parks a line where it sits, in grey italics, with the day or event it comes back on. It comes back by itself. | Nothing waits for ever |
| The one push | Lock-screen notification, only when he alone can do it and it cannot wait for his next sitting. Each one goes in the week with its cause. | Trust in the silence |
| Your week (one level down) | What finished, against plan; what went wrong after he accepted it and what changed; rules learned, each with *take back*; 31 decided for him, 2 worth a look; what now goes ahead without him. | Trust |
| Behind this (one level down) | Three computers, described rather than named, and the kinds of helper on each (making, checking, watching); what each thing cost; stalls caught and restarted. | Curiosity; silence can be checked |
| Out of sight | The counterpart, who writes the page and never does the work; the doers; a checker who never checks their own work; a watcher who restarts helpers that go quiet; the record, which holds every line, promise and rule. | All three |

## The ten principles
1. **Attention:** at rest the page fits one phone screen. Only red needs reading. The total minutes come first.
2. **Reacting:** he reacts to what I heard, sketches, files and pages. He points at what is wrong; he never writes a spec.
3. **Intent first:** every new goal comes back as "what I heard" before any work. Where it is placed already says how it was understood.
4. **Decide once:** "Here only, or always?" The second sketch cites the rule. Rules are listed with *take back*.
5. **Believable silence:** the page changes in only three ways: something comes to him, a promise moves, something is done. "Next:" says when. A watcher catches stalls, and they are listed one level down.
6. **Nothing waits:** every question says what happens if he says nothing. For a choice that cannot be undone, nothing happens and he is asked again with the result. Parked lines carry their return.
7. **Show the thing:** the email, the file, the price page, the screens, the bill, each "checked by someone other than its maker". He corrects by pointing.
8. **One doorway:** one page, one field, one kind of push. Helpers and computers sit one level down, never on the page.
9. **Trust per kind:** after three clean acceptances, wording changes go ahead without him. One miss puts exports back to being shown. The week lists both.
10. **Start no more than he can finish:** the page is the cap; if it no longer fits one phone screen, new work waits. **Served worst:** the cap is a rule behind the page that he cannot see or set, and results still pile up while he is away (Monday: four at once).

## The test week, counted from the prototype (13 moments, 14 events)
- **Places he must look:** 1, the page. Your week and Behind this are one level down from it.
- **Interruptions:** 1 (event 8, the release sign-in).
- **Held in his head between sittings:** 0 pieces of work. One habit remains: his two sitting times, 8:30 and 17:00.
- **Words read:** about 1,800 over 9 visits, at most about 500 in one sitting (Tue 17:00), plus about 340 in Your week. Behind this, about 300, is optional. I counted the shown text, labels and buttons included.
- **Words typed:** 0. Spoken: 23, in two sentences (events 2 and 11).
- **Taps:** 35, plus 8 page opens (the ninth visit was opened from the notification), plus 2 if he looks behind.
- **Decisions:** 18, of which only he could make 12. The other 6 are five acceptances a check could carry and the go on the export fix. They are the next things to trust.
- **Minutes:** about 37 in sittings, 3 in Your week, plus 2 if he looks behind.

## Left out, and what goes next
Left out: counts, badges and app-icon numbers; progress and stages (building, checking); cycles and par on the page; any feed or history; search, settings and a project picker; helper and computer names; a thread for each thing; a pile of "answer later".
Next to cut: the sentence under the top line listing names (the red ink already shows them); "done" lines, which could leave the page at once instead of the next morning; the "Show more" link on files.

## Three weakest points
1. A calm page that is always true is still worth a glance. "Next: … at 17:00" and "Same as when you looked" make a glance return nothing, but cannot remove the habit. A prototype cannot prove this.
2. It holds about 12 lines. With more goals, I must hold back or park more. Past that point the picture becomes the long list it was meant to replace.
3. During a sitting on the phone, the open thing pushes the rest of the page off the screen, so he cannot see the picture while he acts. Sittings also carry a lot of reading (about 500 words at Tue 17:00).

## Where I departed from the premise
- **"Everything he asked for":** done lines leave the next morning for Your week. Choices made for him never appear. Work done without him shows only "done", with no note.
- **"Where each thing stands":** each line shows only when he will next see it, never the stage. A stage is movement that needs nothing from him.
- **"Set apart at the top":** set apart by the top sentence and red ink, not by a second copy of the lines. Each line stays in its place.
- **"Acts where it sits":** on the desk the thing opens on the facing page, tied to its underlined line, because a sketch or a file needs the room.
- **"Never pushed":** one kind of thing is pushed, to the lock screen (event 8).
- **A thing with no place:** the proposal about how I work sits under the top sentence, under "How I work".
