# One true picture

**The idea.** One page, set like a book's table of contents. It holds everything he has asked for, in his words, each in a fixed place, and shows only when he will next see each thing and how recently all of it was checked. The few things that are with him are marked in red ink. The top sentence says what is with him, or when the page will next have anything for him, so looking early tells him nothing new.

## Components

| Component | What it is for | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| The page | Every goal in his words, under the thing it belongs to (Driving app, Billing tool…), in a place that never moves. Each line shows only a promise: a day ("Thu", "sketch Thu"), "parked: after sign-up", "stopped", or "done". | Sight |
| Freshness | The running head says "checked 8:29". When a watcher goes down, the affected lines are hatched and marked "unchecked". The top sentence then says "I can't vouch for all of this page" and never "nothing needs you". | Sight |
| The top sentence | "Three things are with you, about 8 minutes", or "Nothing needs you. Next: …, **at 17:00 today**." "Same as when you looked" if nothing changed. After time away it says what changed, with "What I did while you were away, unedited" underneath. | Sight; works against checking |
| Red ink and Begin | Red is the only colour on the page and means only "with you". Begin goes through those things in the order where delay costs most. | Sight |
| The thing, opened where it sits | On the phone it opens under its line; on the desk, on the facing page. It is one of four kinds: what I heard (the agreement), a sketch, the result itself, or a choice. Every result lists "I assumed" and "Not checked". | Intent; trust |
| If you say nothing | Every question ends in a box. **Mine to do**: the default acts at a stated time, inside authority he gave (named, with *take that back*). **It waits for you**: anything outside that authority, or anything that cannot be undone, is held, and the goal waits. | Trust |
| The evidence | Under every summary, without my edits: the checker's own report, all 17 questions and how each was settled, his voice note word for word, the record of each line. *Ask the checker yourself* goes around me. | Sight; trust |
| Pointing | He taps a line, a paragraph or a row, picks or says what is wrong, then answers once: *here only, or always?* | Intent |
| Stop, undo, take back | One tap on any line gives: change something, not now, stop, undo (once done), and what he has let me decide there, each item with *take back*. *Pause all work* is in Behind this. | Trust |
| Notes told once | One line under the line it concerns: a promise moved (the old day struck through), a question withdrawn, back from parked, a stretch when lines went unchecked. | Sight |
| Say what you want | One field and a mic. The place where the new line lands is how I say back what I understood. | Intent |
| The one push | Lock screen only, and only when he alone can do it and it cannot wait for his next sitting. Each push is counted in his week with its cause. | Trust |
| Your week; Behind this | One level down. Your week: extra rounds marked as "worth it" or "my miss", what he has let me do (he said so) kept apart from what I think he prefers (correct me), and what was unknown and for how long. Behind this: computers, kinds of helpers, two watchers who check each other, a backup counterpart, the cost of each thing. | Trust |

Off screen: the counterpart, who never does the work; the doers; a checker who never checks their own work; two watchers; the record.

## The ten principles
1. **Attention:** when nothing is open, the page fits one phone screen. Only red needs reading, and the total minutes come first. Freshness, defaults and evidence add reading; evidence stays folded until he opens it.
2. **Reacting:** he reacts to agreements, sketches, files and pages. He can still add a constraint in his own words on any line ("Say it…").
3. **Intent first:** every new goal comes back as "what I heard", with done-lines as checkable examples. Placement says how it was understood. A fix reopens it.
4. **Decide once:** "Here only, or always?" Rules he gave are kept apart from preferences I inferred, which are re-asked in a new context.
5. **Believable silence:** the page changes only when something comes to him, a promise moves, or something is done. It shows when it was last checked. Unchecked is shown as unchecked. Two watchers check each other.
6. **Nothing waits silently:** every question says what happens if he says nothing. A default acts only inside authority he gave; otherwise the goal waits. Silence never widens what I may do.
7. **Show the thing:** the email, the file, the page, the screens. "Not checked" is listed. The checker's unedited report is one tap away. A second check does not guarantee quality.
8. **One doorway:** one page, one field, one kind of push. But he can always read past me, ask the checker directly, and a backup counterpart takes over if I stop.
9. **Trust per kind:** the offer comes only after repeated outcomes (12 wording changes still stand), keeps one in five as a random spot-check, resets on change or on one miss, and never covers what cannot be taken back.
10. **Start no more than he can finish:** the limit is his minutes and the age of what waits (about 10 minutes a sitting, nothing older than a day), not a count. **Served worst:** the limit is mine, not something he sets, and while he was away results still waited three days.

## The test week, counted from the prototype (14 moments: 14 events, plus the watcher going down)
- **Places he must look:** 1. **Interruptions:** 1 (event 8).
- **Held in his head:** no piece of work. One habit remains: his sitting times.
- **Words read:** about 2,050 over 9 visits, at most about 570 in one sitting (Tue 17:00), plus about 470 in Your week. Unedited evidence (folded), Behind this (about 370) and the 45-word watcher-down glance are optional. Counts include labels and buttons.
- **Words typed:** 0. Spoken: 23. **Taps:** 36, plus 8 page opens.
- **Decisions:** 19, of which only he could make 13. **Minutes:** about 38, plus 3 for Your week.

## Left out, and what goes next
Left out: badges and counts, stages and progress, cycles and par on the page, feeds, search, settings, a project picker, helper or computer names, a thread for each thing, an "answer later" pile.
Next to cut: the list of names under the top sentence; "done" lines lingering until the next morning; the "Show more" link on file previews.

## Three weakest points
1. A calm, true page still invites a glance. "Next … at 17:00" and "same as when you looked" make the glance return nothing; they cannot stop the habit.
2. It holds about 12 lines. With more goals I must hold back or park more, or it turns into a long list.
3. Sittings carry a lot of reading. The authority and "Not checked" lines made them longer (about 570 words on Tuesday at 17:00), and on the phone the open thing pushes the picture off the screen.

## Where I departed from the premise, and why
- **"Everything he asked for":** done lines leave for Your week the next morning. Choices made for him appear only as evidence.
- **"Where each thing stands":** only when he will next see it, never the stage. I added one status, the check time, because silence has to be able to say "unknown".
- **"Set apart at the top":** set apart by the top sentence and red ink, not by a second copy of the lines.
- **"Acts where it sits":** on the desk the thing opens on the facing page, because a sketch or file needs the room.
- **"Never pushed":** one kind of thing is pushed, to the lock screen.
