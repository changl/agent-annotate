# One conversation: a thread with only a present tense

**The idea.** He talks with one counterpart, and the conversation keeps nothing on screen but its present turn: one sentence that is always true, the one thing in front of him, and his answer at his thumb. What is settled folds to a line behind it; what he is owed waits ahead.

How it beats the long scroll. Five rules, all in the prototype:
1. Nothing open ever lives in the past. An unanswered thing is carried into the present each time; scrolling back never finds work.
2. The counterpart speaks only when something needs him or he asked. Told-once news rides on the next sitting and leaves as soon as he answers anything.
3. One thing at a time, in the order of what delay costs. Each ends with what happens if he does not answer.
4. A settled thing becomes one line in his own words, a day one line, a week the ledger. After three days away he sees the same one sentence, three told lines and three things: current in about nine minutes.
5. Answers to his own questions (who is working where, what a goal cost) vanish when closed. They are not history.

Behind the screen: the record (every goal, agreement, ruling and result); the rule book (answers kept so they are never asked twice); the checker (never the doer); the watcher (catches a silent helper); the gate (orders by cost of delay, keeps at most seven things on him, applies the knock test); the trust table (for each kind of work: shown, sampled, or done and reported).

## The ten principles

1. Attention: one prepared thing at a time, recommended answer first. 18 decisions took 31 taps.
2. Reacting: he never writes. He touches a line and a margin note. Words typed in the week: 0.
3. Agree first: the agreement is a sheet he says Go to. Its one open question rides on the Go button itself.
4. Decide once: each receipt names the rule kept. Wednesday's agreement and showing already use Tuesday's rulings.
5. Silence: lamps out and no colour mean all is well. The night stall changes nothing on screen. The mistake is owned up to first thing.
6. Nothing waits silently: every sheet ends "If I do not hear", with a time and what waiting costs.
7. Show the thing: rows of the file, the email, the screens; checked by another helper; corrected by pointing.
8. One doorway: one surface, one line to speak into. Projects, helpers and computers appear only if he asks.
9. Trust: a proposal after three first-time acceptances; the mistake narrows money work back to "shown with real rows".
10. Pace: "I started nothing new while you were away"; never more than seven things on him. **Served worst:** nothing in this week tests the cap, so it is stated, not felt. Close second is 7: a result bigger than a sheet has to be opened outside.

## Left out, and what I would cut next

Left out: project pages; a list of questions; status; helper and computer names up front; search or browsing of the past (he asks instead); settings; counts on an icon; any notification but the knock; spoken replies (voice in is drawn, voice out is not built).
Cut next: the lock-screen sentence, if it nags; the "Then" list under the sheet; the Settled column on the desk, which could be one tap away as on the phone.

## Three weakest points

1. An agreement is more than two phone screens long. Go is always in reach, so he can agree without reading the "done" lines, and the intent gap stays open.
2. A result bigger than a sheet (a running app, a 212-row file) is opened outside. Pointing at what is wrong out there, and bringing the mark back, is not solved.
3. One thing at a time is slow when five small things wait, and hides how they bear on each other. And with no ping, a default can fire before he has sat down; he learns of it afterwards.

## Where I left the premise, and why

- **It is not a transcript.** A spoken conversation keeps no visible history; a written one does, and that is the scroll. Principles 1 and 5.
- **Two fixed places inside the one surface:** the standing sentence and "In hand". A pure exchange would make him ask "how are things?" every time; principle 5 wants the truth at one glance.
- **The knock leaves the conversation** for the lock screen (principle 6). It is allowed only when all three hold: only he can do it, waiting has a stated cost, and the deadline falls before his next sitting. Each knock is reported with its cause and the change that stops that kind knocking again, and he can point at "this could have waited".
- **The thing itself sometimes lives outside** (weak point 2). Principle 7 outranks "no second place".
