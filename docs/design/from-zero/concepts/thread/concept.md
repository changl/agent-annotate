# One conversation: a thread with only a present tense

**The idea.** He talks with one counterpart, and the conversation keeps nothing on screen but its present turn: one sentence that is always true and says how old its news is, the one thing in front of him, and his answer at his thumb. What is settled folds to a line behind it; what he is owed waits ahead.

How it beats the long scroll. Five rules, all in the prototype:
1. Nothing open ever lives in the past. An unanswered thing is carried into the present each time; scrolling back never finds work.
2. The counterpart speaks only when something needs him or he asked. Told-once news rides on the next sitting and leaves as soon as he answers anything.
3. One thing at a time, in the order of what delay costs. Each ends with what happens if he does not answer. "It goes ahead" appears only inside something he has already allowed, named on the sheet and takeable back there. Everything else says "It waits for you": silence is not a yes.
4. A settled thing becomes one line in his own words, with Undo; a day one line; a week the ledger. After three days away he sees the same one sentence, four told lines and three things: current in about nine minutes.
5. Answers to his own questions vanish when closed. They are not history.

Behind the screen: the record; the rule book (each ruling with its scope, takeable back; a ruling that was only him taking my pick is held loosely); the checker (never the doer, given the "done" lines before the maker's conclusion, able to write to him directly); two watchers, the second watching the first; the gate (orders by cost of delay, starts nothing new once what waits on him passes 10 minutes or 2 days, applies the knock test); the trust table (for each kind of work: shown, sampled, or done and reported).

## The ten principles, as limited by the outside evidence

1. Attention: one prepared thing at a time, recommended answer first. 18 decisions took 31 taps. The evidence cost reading: 3,015 words, up from 2,418.
2. Reacting: he reacts first, by touching a line. What "done" means and one example are written by the counterpart and kept. Words he typed: 0.
3. Agree first: a sheet he says Go to, its open question on the Go button itself. An agreement can be reopened on a new fact; the ledger reports that apart from waste.
4. Decide once: each receipt names the rule kept. Any rule or allowance can be taken back where it is used and in the ledger.
5. Silence: lamps out and no colour mean all is well only while the news is fresh. Otherwise the screen says "I cannot see".
6. Nothing waits silently: every sheet ends "If I do not hear", then "It goes ahead" (inside an allowance) or "It waits for you" (everything else).
7. Show the thing: rows, the email, the screens, plus what was not checked and the checker's own words. Thursday's mistake was a summary that dropped a "not checked".
8. One doorway, with a way round it: the record, and a checker's direct word.
9. Trust: proposed only after three in a row that also stayed right for two weeks; one in five still shown at random; ends on a miss or a change; never for what cannot be undone.
10. Pace: the limit is minutes and age (10 minutes, 2 days), not a count. **Served worst: 7.** A result bigger than a sheet has to be opened outside, and pointing at it there is not solved.

## Left out, and what I would cut next

Left out: project pages; a list of questions; status; helper and computer names up front; search or browsing of the past (he asks instead); settings; counts on an icon; any notification but the knock; spoken replies (voice in is drawn, voice out is not built); a half-blind state where only some goals are unseen.
Cut next: the "Then" list under the sheet; the Settled column on the desk, which could be one tap away as on the phone; the "For example" line once a kind of work is familiar.

## Three weakest points

1. The recommended answer is the big button and Go is always in reach, on an agreement over two phone screens long. The design makes agreeing easier than reading, and his taking my pick will look like his preference.
2. A result bigger than a sheet (a running app, a 212-row file) is opened outside. Pointing at what is wrong out there, and bringing the mark back, is not solved.
3. The way round the counterpart lives inside the same surface. If the surface itself is down there is no second door, and "I cannot see" depends on the phone keeping honest time.

## Where I left the premise, and why

- **It is not a transcript.** A spoken conversation keeps no visible history; a written one does, and that is the scroll. Principles 1 and 5.
- **Two fixed places inside the one surface:** the standing sentence and "In hand". A pure exchange would make him ask "how are things?" every time; principle 5 wants the truth, and its age, at one glance.
- **The knock leaves the conversation** for the lock screen (principle 6). Allowed only when all three hold: only he can do it, waiting has a stated cost, and the deadline falls before his next sitting. Each knock is reported with its cause and the change that stops that kind knocking again.
- **A door that goes round the one counterpart** (the record, a checker's direct word), and **the thing itself outside**. A single doorway must not be a single point of failure, and principle 7 outranks "no second place".
