# v2-return: coming back and staying in control

**What I built.** One component, nine moments, 26 states, in `index.html`. Every state is two facing pages: on the left what is so, on the right what he can do about it. On the phone they stack, and nothing is pinned over the content, so an answer cannot be reached before the thing it answers. `#check` walks every state and 14 side paths and reports on screen. It also fails on any word of the method, any control under 44 px, any sideways scroll, and on "nothing needs you" shown while work is unseen or waiting. `#count` counts. `#m=3&s=2&t=dark` opens one state.

| Moment | What he gets | What it needs from its host |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Back after five days | One sentence on the lock screen, with the bad news in it. Then "what changed", by consequence (went wrong, lost, decided for you with undo, moved, not begun), under one bar that adds up to 40. **Taken from each concept.** Sitting: the oversized return split into "now" and dated remainders, with minutes; his words in serif and mine in sans. Letter: the phone working out staleness by itself; a refusal on the interruption; the checker's doubt in the checker's words; take back beside the thing. Picture: doubt marked only on the goals it touches, hatched; a moved date struck through on its own goal; two facing pages on the desk; rules he gave kept apart from guesses. Thread: undo on every receipt and on every choice made for him; the record that does not pass through me; the consequence written inside the answer.

**The three problems.**
1. *Showing a thing that moves*: not answered here; it was not my part. Two joints are ready for it. Every result on these pages says who checked it, against which "done" lines, and what was not checked. And the trace ties a wrong result to what was built on it and to what already left.
2. *Coming back*: answered for five days away, a thin week, a bad day, a wrong rule and a lost grant. Each is a bounded page that ends in one or two prepared choices. He is never asked to keep the rule book: rules lapse unused, and a rule used outside the case it came from comes to him with its uses already sorted.
3. *Rhythm*: partly. There are no fixed hours. "Goes ahead" is a clock time. "Cannot wait" means cannot wait until the day I next expect him, which is learnt from him and shown. An interruption is tried twice, on two devices, then stops and is accounted for. Pulling one thing early is drawn only as moving it into "now".

Off the path: the allowed page, the missed interruption and its account, the five blind states, the five stop states.

**Three weakest points.**
1. The return is bounded for each opening, not in total. Had all forty goals finished while he was away, the same page would offer eight openings. It names the debt and stops it growing. It cannot shrink it.
2. Every page rests on things the screen cannot show to be true: that the 19 "as promised" were really seen, that the draw cannot be steered, that nothing can be released while nobody is looking. I state them. Only use will prove them.
3. A bad day is still 431 words and four decisions, and the wrong rule hides a second choice behind the first. On the desk the second page is often half empty.

**Of the nine, not fully honest and bounded.**
- 1, the return: bounded per opening only (weakest point 1).
- 4, the wrong rule: he finds out only when a check or a customer notices. A rule that is wrong quietly, inside its own case, never surfaces.
- 5, what he has allowed: calm at 30 rules by grouping. His record holds about 1,600 procedures; at that size it is a place to search, not to read.
- 8, whole-blind: it depends on his phone keeping time after the last word it got. If the phone itself is off, nothing says so.

**What in revision 2 still cannot be drawn.**
- Principle 12 defines defaults and "cannot wait" against the next sitting. A man who opens it twice a week has no next sitting. I drew "when I next expect you", learnt from him. The method should say this.
- Principle 10: work is admitted at his rate and he is told what is held. Which of forty goals to slow is a choice of priority that the method gives to nobody. I drew only "not begun: 3".
- Principle 6: a default acts only on the side that can be undone. Thirteen undoable choices in five days build on each other. Undoing the first after others rest on it is the same trace problem as a wrong result; the method treats undo as free.
- Principle 5, "something watches the watch": I can draw the phone noticing silence. I cannot draw a watch that is wrong while still talking.
- The limit itself (10 minutes, 2 days): the method does not say who sets it, or how he changes it.
