# The showing: one sheet for every kind of work

I built one part in depth: **the showing**, a sheet with its own edges that a letter-like or a sitting-like surface can hold. It covers 13 real examples across all 12 kinds of work, on phone and desk, light and dark. To try it, open `index.html`. The sheet is shown alone, full width; the striped bar is outside the design: it picks the kind, "marks it for me", brings a sent-back result back, and breaks the seal. `#check` presses through 68 states and reports on screen. It fails a state on any word of the method on screen, or any control under 44 by 44 px (the parts of the thing he points at are exempt on purpose: words, a map, a page's own elements). `#s=I&mode=act` opens any state directly.

## What is on the sheet (the same parts for every kind)
- **The seal.** One line: "Checked by someone else · this exact build, on an iPhone 13 · recorded 16:10". Build numbers and fingerprints stay in the record, not on the screen. What he looks at, marks and accepts is that same thing. If anything changes after the check, the seal breaks, goes red and dashed, and accepting is disabled until it is checked again.
- **The thing itself, in its own colours, both themes.** Anything that moves comes as a *take*: a recording of the exact build, made by the checker and played or scrubbed inside the sheet (a journey, a route). Everything else is shown as its real form:
  - the served page at both widths, usable: switch the price, press Start;
  - words in the email as received;
  - the picture at full size, beside today's version and in place on a home screen;
  - only the changed rows, plus a fresh read-back from the live shop;
  - "sameness" for work with nothing to see: before and after, an allowed and a refused case, a count;
  - a number as its ten runs before and ten after;
  - a job as one real run plus a failure planted on purpose;
  - proof as the failing case beside the same case passing;
  - a comparison with every figure dated;
  - what goes and what stays.
- **Yours to judge.** One question only he can answer, first on the phone. Where nothing can be judged by eye it says so and names the one trade-off that is his.
- **Done lines · four pass.** One verb, "checked", throughout. Each done line is a number; it jumps to its moment on the scrubber, or outlines its element, row or step in the thing; the checker's own words are one tap further. On a phone, the numbers are all he has to read.
- **"Looks right" is kept apart from what was checked.** The button records his judgement only and names no version. What was not checked is said once, above the yes, before he decides: "Not checked: old phones. Your yes does not cover it." Where accepting is itself the act, the button says the act: "Yes, these prices from 1 Dec", "Remove the 214".
- **Pointing.** Mark freezes the thing, and nothing in it can be used by accident while he points. He taps a control in a frame, a spot on the route (the mark keeps place and minute), an element at either width, a word (a second tap stretches the selection), a spot on a picture, a row or a single price, a cell, or a step in a trace. "The whole thing" is always there, for numbers and feel. A chip from the kind's own list ends a mark; chips ending in "…" open the say field. Each mark keeps its anchor: "on 'Pay now' at 0:56"; the version is kept with it in the record. "Just this once, or always?" is asked once, at Send back.
- **The round trip.** Sent back: "comes back checked again by 17:00". The second version opens with "Your two marks: all dealt with". Each of his pins sits where he put it, now green with a tick, with **Before / Now** for that exact spot: the old frame, the old route dashed under the new, struck words beside new words. He sees at once that his mark was dealt with, without reading a report.
- **The pause before what cannot be undone** (release; switching texts on for real):
  - the exact thing and what is in it;
  - to whom, as a choice, and the sentence said back changes with it ("Release Driving app 2.4 to 1 in 10 iPhone users today at 14:00…");
  - what cannot be undone and what can;
  - that his yes holds for this exact build only ("the build you saw on Friday");
  - press and hold "Correct. Release it." (keyboard: hold Space);
  - then a receipt, and "Stop it reaching anyone else".

## What it needs from its host
In: the goal in his words; the done lines; the checked revision and the checker's record; how long it should take to look; the authority he has given (for "mine to do / waits for you" lines); his next sitting (for "if you say nothing"); voice capture for "Say it…". Room: on the desk a region of 960 px or more (the thing, plus a 330 px rail); on the phone, full width and the bottom edge for the answer bar, with the host's "back" above the sheet. Out: accepted (revision); marks (revision, anchor, reason, words, once or always); act confirmed (revision, audience, said-back sentence, receipt); answers to forks; take back. The host must **never swap the version under him** while the sheet is open; it may say "a newer version is ready". The host owns timing, the one interruption, rules and where things come back.

## Taken from each concept
Letter: the enclosure in its own colours, marks on the thing, the checker's own words, take back on a mark. Sitting: "only you can judge" first, choices shown as the real alternatives, judgement kept apart from checks. Thread: chips specific to the problem, answers within thumb reach, the keyboard. Picture (mine): the desk spread (thing plus rail), a seal that breaks in plain sight, as hatched lines did, struck-through change, red ink only for what is his.

## The three unsolved problems
1. **Things that move: answered for showing and correcting, with one honest gap.** Takes, two widths, a played route, a job's real run, a number's runs; marks keep frame, time, width or word; the seal ties what he marks to what was checked. Gap: feel in the hand, sound, GPS and real speed cannot be judged from a recording. "Try it in your hand" opens the same build on his phone and promises that marks made there come back. That is a second surface, and its return is promised, not drawn.
2. **Coming back: only what a sheet can carry.** After any absence a showing still opens on the exact version, with his marks kept, and a sent-back result comes back answered in place. Reopening an accepted result and tracing what was built on it is the host's job and is not drawn here.
3. **Rhythm: not answered here.** The sheet states its look time so the host can fit sittings to minutes, and takes "next sitting" from the host for anything that waits.

## The stress week, counted from the prototype (the showings only)
The week has 9 showings for 7 goals: sign-up (marked, then the second version), price page, photos, test run, welcome email (marked, then the second version), reminder texts (plus switching them on), and Thursday's release (the pause). Word counts come from `#check`.
- **Places to look:** 1, the sheet inside its host. Optional: the build on his phone. **Interruptions:** 0 from showings; the release pause sits in a sitting.
- **Held in his head:** nothing. Marks wait on the exact version; the return time is shown. The habit of his sittings belongs to the host.
- **Words he must read to act:** 1,445 on the phone and 1,567 on the desk. This includes the things' own words (email, price rows) and every label and button. **Available but not required:** about 690 more (proof details, the checker's own words).
- **Typed:** 0. **Spoken:** about 17 words (three marks). **Taps:** 30 (sign-up 11, email 11, release 2, texts 3, price, photos and test run 1 each).
- **Decisions:** 14. Only his: 12 (four marks, two "once or always", three judgements of taste, switching texts on for real, the release). Acceptances of what a check had passed: 2 (photos, test run), each with a trade-off the agreement could have settled.
- **Minutes:** about 7, within each sheet's own look time (30 s to 1 min; the decision sheet 2 min).

## Kinds I could not show honestly, and why
- **Phone app and car:** feel, timing in the hand, sound, GPS and driver distraction. A recording or simulator is labelled as such, and "driven in a real car" stays not proved.
- **Behind the app:** the absence of leaks. Only an allowed and a refused pair can be shown.
- **Job:** outcomes that need weeks (fewer missed lessons).
- **Release:** what users do after release.
- **Prices:** what prices do to sales.
- **Cleanup:** whether anything outside the app refers to what is removed.
- **Decision:** whether the sources stay true.
- **Words:** whether a real inbox files the email as junk.

Each is said once, above the yes, as "Not checked: …" with its reason, never as a pass.

## Three weakest points
1. Takes are recordings. They cannot be explored, and anything not recorded cannot be marked. The device handoff leaves the doorway.
2. The safeguard lines still cost words when they have nothing new to say: about 25 to 35 per sheet (the seal and the "not checked" line above the yes).
3. Desk-width pages shown at a third of their size are small targets for pointing. "Desk, larger" is one tap, but it is one more step.

## What revision 2 still cannot be drawn
- "The check must be able to fail" only shows where a failure can be planted (proof, jobs). For taste kinds, there is nothing to plant.
- "A random sample is still shown" needs the host to say "shown at random"; the sheet cannot know.
- Accepting a known gap ("your yes does not cover") has no word in the method; it should.
- Timing and feel ("too slow between step 2 and 3") can only be pointed at as a moment on the scrubber.
