# The sheet: one design from the best of four

**What I built.** One sheet. It is on his lock screen as a glance and on his desk as the left page of an open book. He sits at two times he set (8:30 and 5:00), but it opens at any time. Each sitting is bounded: the things, their minutes, their order, and it ends with "That is all." Each thing opens on the one sentence only he can judge, shows the thing itself, and keeps the proof apart. What happens if he leaves a thing is written in words. Everything else is silent behind a glance that carries its age and names what it cannot see.

Open `index.html`. "next" in the striped bar steps through 15 moments of the stress week; "notes" says what he does not see. Under 1040 px it is the phone; above, the desk. `#check` runs every state and reports on screen. `#k=back&th=signup` and the like open one state.

## The three unsolved problems

**1. A thing that moves.** Answered in part.
- *A journey* (Faster sign-up): the three screens run inside the sheet and he taps through them. A switch separates "Use it" from "Mark it", so pointing never presses a button. He marks a spot on a screen, or the wait between two screens; each mark keeps the screen it was made on. It cannot be accepted until he has walked it.
- *A page at two widths* (Price page): one page, laid out by the room it is given, at phone width and at 1280. Its own buttons work. Marks keep their width. It cannot be accepted until both were seen. On the desk it can take the whole spread; on the phone the desk width is a small picture and says so.
- *A job with nothing to look at* (Move photos): told, not asked. What ran, line by line, each line markable, what was not tried, and Undo until the day the old copies are deleted. That day cannot be undone and pauses for him.
- *A number* (the test run): the figure, what it was, the spread of ten runs in words, and Undo.
- *What cannot be undone* (the release, the deletion): the exact thing, to whom, what cannot be taken back, said back in one sentence, and a held press. A sign-in later is only a sign-in.
- *Tied together*: every thing says which build or file it is and that the checks ran on that one; marks come back beside the next version with what was done about each.
- **Not answered:** a route, a drive, sound, a real payment. The sheet lists them as "Not tried". "Running here as on a phone like yours" is a claim the prototype cannot prove. A mark cannot yet hold a moment inside an animation.

**2. Coming back.** Answered for the cases drawn.
- *Five days away*: still one sheet. First "What the wait cost" (two lines, honest). Then "Went ahead, inside what you allowed", each with Undo. Then "Held": six goals not started, named one touch away. Then nine things, 26 minutes, split across three sittings, the costliest three first.
- *A week of two sittings*: no nagging. It slowed what starts, says what it held, and asks one question: is this your pace now?
- *A rule turned out wrong* (day 30): the rule comes to him only because it was stretched. His own words, the nine times it was used, the one that was wrong, what was done. One choice: where it applies. He curates nothing.
- *An accepted result found bad, with work built on it*: the miss comes first, with "Built on it": each goal that used it and what was done.
- *Trust lost*: told, not asked, with the cause.
- **Not answered:** the six things deferred on the return are named but not drawn. A trace longer than two goals has no drawn bound.

**3. Rhythm.** Answered.
- He sits at 8:30 and 5:00. "Goes ahead at 5:00" means at the next sitting time, whether or not he sits; it is then told once on the goal's line, with Undo.
- He pulls early three ways: open the sheet between sittings and take what is "Ready early"; "Say it back now" after speaking; "Start it now" on a goal's page.
- The one interruption: only he can do it, it cannot wait for 5:00, a named loss. It says why it could not wait and that it is the first in three weeks. The week counts it against me, with what I changed.

## Three weakest points

1. **The moving thing is still a stand-in.** What runs in the sheet is Friday's build "as on a phone like yours". Anything that needs the real device, road or card is only listed as not tried, so a release can rest on proof he never saw.
2. **Told-not-asked leans wholly on the check.** For work with nothing to look at I chose fewer empty acceptances over a second pair of eyes. If the checker shares the maker's blind spot, he finds out at the undo deadline.
3. **It is still a lot of reading, and Go can be pressed unread.** The agreement is the longest item (about 190 words). The open question must be answered before Go, but nothing shows he read the done lines. On the desk the facing page shows his forty goals by default, which invites checking.

## What in revision 2 still cannot be drawn

- **Is a sitting a time or an act?** Defaults are "defined against the next sitting". I had to choose the clock ("whether or not you sit"). If he opens the sheet at 4:50, reads and leaves, the method does not say.
- **Admitting work at his rate (10).** I can draw "held" and "I slowed down". The method gives no order among forty goals, so I cannot draw which six are held or why those.
- **A kind of work (9).** Trust is by kind, but nobody names the kinds or their edges. My day-30 miss is a "small wording change" that changed what people did. The edge can only be put in words.
- **Showing a goal with nothing to judge (7, method step "showing").** Every goal ends in "accept, or point". For a background job that is a rubber stamp. The method should say when a result is told instead.
- **The exact result (7).** I can print which build the checks ran on. He cannot see that it is true.
- **Cannot see, for a long time (5).** The method says what the glance shows, not what happens next. I invented: first line of the next sheet; reach him once if only he can fix it.
