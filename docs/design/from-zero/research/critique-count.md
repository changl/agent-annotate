## The frozen rule and its limits

I wrote `/tmp/count-rule.txt` before opening the prototypes, then opened each before reading its designer's account. A place means a distinct content surface, not an app or URL; another card in the same stream is not another place. Thread's five are its turn, fleet reply, goal reply, proposed whole-file viewer and proposed sign-in; Sitting's six are door, sitting, glance, fleet, goal and sign-in; Picture's four are picture, week, behind and sign-in; Letter's six are glance, letter, enclosure, week, behind and sign-in. Proposed external surfaces are charged even when their implementation is missing.

An interruption is delivery he did not choose to open, regardless of sound. Thread adds a return preview to its urgent knock. Letter exposes five letter previews across Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, the away interval and Monday, plus the urgent buzz. These are designed exposures, not six observed phone notifications; live delivery was not tested. If a preview is explicitly opt-in, its interruption charge disappears, but that preference is not established here.

Memory counts distinct obligations between sittings, including habits. All four require keeping their opening rhythm. Sitting additionally requires declaring an absence; its eight-word declaration and its input path are charged. Persisted goals and return conditions are not counted as remembered facts. Zero facts assumes the promised record actually persists; the prototypes do not establish that.

Words use Unicode letters/digits, retaining internal apostrophes and hyphens; punctuation alone is not a word. Read the current agreement, owner choices and consequences, subjective copy, assumptions, uncovered cases, confirmation and the requested ledger information. Already checked sample table cells and optional raw reports are available, not required reading. A repeated unchanged line within one sitting is not automatically reread; required rereading at another sitting is charged. Ignore prototype narration, counters, keyboard hints and inactive folded items. Available exposures count each distinct reached visible product state once, including repeated surrounding text; they are not a unique vocabulary or a demand to read everything.

The common correction is “Too long. Make it shorter and warmer.” (7 words, 37 characters), then Always. Picture's two supplied chips express that correction without typing; its custom “Say it…” path merely records “in your words”. The walking goal is 11 spoken words. Picture's error report is 12 typed words, 67 characters. Simulated recognition is charged as owner speech, never as free intent or as typing too.

An activation is one press; each entered character, space and punctuation mark is a key, plus an actual submission key. Launcher openings and the two missing entry presses before Picture's pre-opened parking menu are charged; time-jump controls and diagnostic rehearsals are not. Phone-only navigation is shown separately. Scroll reveals observed in the desk replay were at least 2/2/3/6; these are not a complete physical scrolling study. Consequently the time totals are lower bounds, not measured human weeks.

A decision is a semantic commitment: a new goal, agreement, embedded owner fork, correction, scope, park, acceptance or delegation. Three answers signed together remain three decisions. Begin, Next, Done and opening evidence are navigation. A default inside previously granted authority is not an only-owner decision merely because it is labelled “your choice”; checked-result acceptances are counted separately. This catches the needless yearly-price commitment in all four and Picture's extra repair agreement.

Minutes = required words/220 + typed words/35 + spoken words/120 + activations/60 + observed scrolls/60 + ordinary decisions×5/60 + only-owner decisions×15/60 + three visual comparisons×10/60 + interruptions×30/60 + 30/60 for sign-in. The same assumptions apply to all four. At 60 rather than 15 seconds per only-owner decision, add 10.5/12/11.25/10.5 minutes. Authentication difficulty, real app trials, delivery failures and recovery remain unpriced.

No added goal or fork earns a saving or penalty in the common commitment count: I excluded Sitting's extra wording examples and newly arrived tax agreement, and Letter's error-message goal and opt-out fork. Existing goals that a prototype brings back within its week still cost attention. Their different completion dates and fixed ledger totals prevent interpreting these as equal-output productivity measurements.

## What the comparison establishes

1. **Sitting**: clearest finite obligation and best parking; its longer prose can be edited without abandoning the concept.
2. **Letter**: best correction mechanics and low navigation; the daily preview and OS dependence are real additional obligations.
3. **Thread**: a strong current turn and compact receipts; long agreements and the unsolved external-result return weaken it.
4. **Picture**: best optional orientation; poorest main doorway once exception scanning grows toward forty goals.
This ranking concerns the interaction, not the smallest modeled minute count or a sum of principle scores.

I would build Sitting's finite opening and ending, Letter's markable enclosure and checker voice, Picture's optional named-goal overview, and Thread's terse settled receipts. The real differences are location of correction, required navigation, unsolicited surfaces and the habit the system demands. The 139-word Thread/Picture reversal demonstrates how much of the advertised difference was omitted destinations and authored prose. Differences caused by scheduling more results into this week are deferred work, not intrinsic attention savings.

The method needs correction too: an agreement cannot freeze all future intent; new facts and discoveries are not automatically waste. A default needs prior authority and a safe consequence, not a high recommendation acceptance rate. Sampling needs continued independent outcomes, not three pleasant approvals. A cap must constrain admitted review minutes and age when the person is absent, with a drawn overload policy. “One doorway” cannot honestly mean one content surface when the person must inspect a real result and its original evidence.

The biggest unsolved problem is proving the quiet sentence from fresh, independent, end-to-end evidence while every component, including its observer and phone delivery, can fail. All four draw that promise; none demonstrates it. Real push, authentication, persistence, running-result comparison and watcher recovery remain unverified, so the numeric attention totals are lower bounds rather than validated weekly budgets.
