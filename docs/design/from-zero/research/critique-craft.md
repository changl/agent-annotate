## 1. Cold, as him

**Thread.** I understand at once that three things wait and the first is to agree to "Driver ratings". I do not know what the three dots are, what "Par 3" means, or that Go also answers the "Only you" question below the fold (its small print says "instructors see ratings, without names"). The greeting is the loudest thing on every step and leaves about 450 px for the work. Warm, editorial, calm. Quotation marks around every goal name make each list look like a page of citations.

**Sitting.** The clearest ten seconds of the four: what, how many, how long, where to start. Then it talks too much: two lines about the watcher and a tested phone on every door, and one sentence I cannot parse. Inside a sitting it looks like a lesson app, not a work tool, which is good. The price decision drawn as two real price cards is the best thing on any phone screen.

**Picture.** Handsome typography, dotted leaders, one red ink. But the first thing I see is my whole portfolio, and most of it needs nothing; this is a status board in a book's clothes. "what I heard", "your choice" and "sketch 17:00" are plain, yet I must learn that a red bar on the left is the only thing that matters.

**Letter.** The phone opens on a lock screen I already own, so on day one I barely see the design at all. Open it and it is the most humane surface here: a dated letter, his words in blue ink, "Take back" beside every answer, "That is all." On the desk the first impression is a grey loading skeleton with the real design squeezed into a 350 px notification in the top right.

## 2. Defects in the screenshots

- **Thread.** At 1280 the sheet is a clipped inner scroll that cuts mid-line behind the answer bar (every sitting state). At 390 the preselected chips ("Half", "After", "Keep" filled black) read as already answered (Tue 4:40 pm). The week item's rows are unevenly spaced (Mon 8:30, phone). The desk shows the knock inside a drawn phone frame.
- **Sitting.** The floating "Say it" pill covers the last row at 390 (Tue 17:05 door, Thu 17:00 door, Mon 9:00 door). The door, glance and end on the desk leave about 350 px empty on each side. The yellow point-mode fill on the email is loud in light mode. Monospace PASS/NOTE in the checker's report looks like a developer tool.
- **Picture.** Dotted leaders collapse to three dots when a name wraps at 390 ("Fix wrong totals on the weekly report", "Reminder texts go out too late"). "Speed up the test run" sits under the say bar on first load. **Wed 13:52, desk:** the push toast covers the end of the headline and the mic, and the red "can't wait" mark sits on *Move photos to cheaper storage*: the release sign-in is filed under the wrong goal (`releaseUrgent: {goal: 'photos'}`).
- **Letter.** At 1280 every lock moment is dominated by a grey skeleton; with the email open, the enclosure overlaps the skeleton's edge (Wed 8:30, marks). Blue ink on near-black is the weakest contrast in any dark theme. The dashed and solid "I'd say" rings differ by about one pixel of stroke.
- **All four, dark mode.** Holds up. Enclosed things (email, invoice) keep their own light paper in dark, which is right.

## 3. Best and worst single screen

**Best: Picture, Thu 9:55, phone, "I can't vouch for all of this page."** The running head turns to "UNCHECKED SINCE 9:14", only the two affected lines are hatched and read "Fri · unchecked", and the headline refuses to say "nothing needs you". It makes doubt local, visible and plain in one glance, which is the hardest thing in the method. The runner-up is Sitting's price page: the decision is the two real pages, with a recommendation and what happens at 5 pm.

**Worst: Picture, Wed 13:52, desk.** The one moment that cannot wait shows its red mark on the wrong goal, and the toast covers its own headline. A close second is Letter's desk at 8:30: the largest object on screen is a placeholder.

## 7. Where each fails in real use

- **Thread.** By day 30, Go with an embedded default is pressed unread. With forty goals the In hand list becomes the board he left. One sheet at a time hides how goals bear on each other. The lock-screen sentence becomes a badge he checks.
- **Sitting.** Everything rests on a rhythm of two sittings a day, yet his record shows a median of one answer per return, 52 minutes after the ask (D2). Skipped sittings turn into 16-minute Mondays. The repeated watcher line turns to wallpaper first, and that is where belief in silence will crack.
- **Picture.** Ten goals become forty, and the page becomes a long status list: the design admits the glance invites checking. Anything that is not a goal (a release sign-in, a trust offer) has no line, so it is misfiled or a pseudo-goal is invented.
- **Letter.** A fixed hour puts a day into every taste loop (sketch, mark, sketch). Anything that moves leaves the letter. If the OS stops refreshing the widget, a stale "Nothing needs you" stays up, the exact failure the design exists to prevent. Miss the ring code once and a default surprises him.
- **All four.** A visual result that needs comparing (two versions of a screen, a map route, a driving behaviour) is shown as stills or sent outside. That is the biggest kind of work in his record: 564 screen or layout acceptances and 232 route or drive choices (D2). A correction that is hard to point at (timing, feel) falls back to typing, the habit the method was meant to retire.

## 8. Worth stealing

Thread: the concrete "For example" scene under the done-lines; Undo on every receipt; plain machine names ("the one on the shelf"); "That, not your accepting them, is the evidence". Sitting: "Only you can judge…" as each item's first line; decisions drawn as real alternatives; the goal page's five verbs; "the first 3 now, the rest at 5 pm"; his words in serif, mine in sans. Picture: hatched unchecked lines; a struck-through old date on the line itself; the desk two-page spread; "you said so" kept apart from "I think you prefer". Letter: the lock-screen glance with "That is not what I said"; strike-through as correction; "Take back" beside every mark; the P.S. for trust offers; the buzz copy ("Not in your letter because…"); "Your phone noticed, not me".

## 9. Ranking

1. **Letter.** The only one that does not look like a work tool. It needs the least reading and has the warmest plain copy at the hard moments. Its desk is undesigned and its hour is too rigid.
2. **Sitting.** The most complete and the most controllable, with the best decision screens. It costs the most words and taps, and it reads like a lesson app.
3. **Thread.** Strong pointing and receipts and a real desk, but it speaks method jargon (par, cycles) and its greeting outshouts the work.
4. **Picture.** It has the best single screen and the best desk, but its front door is a project status board, the thing he rejected, and it misfiles the one urgent moment.

## 10. The combination I would build

The front door is Letter's lock-screen glance (age counted on the phone, "I cannot see", hold to say, "That is not what I said"). It opens a single sheet rewritten in place, as in Letter, but it opens whenever he chooses and at his set times. It is not one fixed hour, so taste loops are not held a day. The sheet keeps Letter's voice and ink. Each item opens with Sitting's "Only you can judge…", decisions are drawn as the real alternatives, and defaults are written in words, not rings ("Goes ahead at 5 pm: you allowed page layout. Take back"). Correction is Letter's strike and pin on a full-size enclosure, plus Thread's margin chips, then "here only, or always?". Told-once news and doubt live on the goal's own line, as in Picture: ~~Thu~~ Fri, and hatched for unchecked. The desk is Picture's spread: the sheet on the left, the thing on the right. Control is Sitting's goal page and Undo on every receipt. The ledger is Sitting's discovery-versus-miss and Picture's "you said so" versus "my guess". One copy rule applies throughout: no par, cycle, watcher, counterpart, spot-check, lamp or knock on any screen he sees.

**What none of the four solved:**

1. Showing and correcting something that moves, without leaving the doorway: a running build on his phone, a screen at two widths, a route.
2. Forty goals.
3. Folding a new requirement he speaks mid-review into the agreement. His record has 42% of typed answers adding a requirement (D2); every concept makes the answer a tap and speech a side door.
4. A visible track record that silence has been right. Only Sitting's "21 of 22 promises kept" begins it.

## 11. What the concepts show about the method

- Its own vocabulary (par, cycles) leaked onto Thread's screens. The method should forbid showing its metrics to him in its own words.
- "No status" and "one glance shows the truth" collide. Every concept had to invent freshness, an "unknown" state and a per-goal line. That glance deserves to be a sixth moment, specified.
- "Decide once" turned single answers into rules. The concepts that matured split rules he gave from guesses. The method should say so.
- "One doorway" cannot be drawn for moving results. The enclosure, or a handoff that brings marks back, belongs in the method, not left to each designer.
- The sitting is an unproven change of habit against a record of one-at-a-time answers. The test week has no flood above the cap, no visual comparison and no forty-goal day, so it cannot catch the failures above.
