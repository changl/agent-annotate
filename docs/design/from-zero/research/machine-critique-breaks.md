# What breaks the four machines

References R/C/O/P mean the four files in `../machine/`, with their numbered sections. Research references mean files beside this one. All detection times and build costs below are proposed limits, not observed performance. “He sees nothing” means successful recovery keeps the promise; recovery still costs effort and money.
This is a design comparison, not a runtime audit. Independent drafting produced agreement, but all four shared the same method and some outside evidence: agreement is useful convergence, not four independent experiments.

## Where all four agree

- One durable authority, replaceable helpers and rebuildable views; a conversation and a board status cannot be the memory (§§2–3,12).
- A checked agreement includes boundaries, examples, authority, next showing and par; discovery can reopen it (§§3,5,14).
- A maker's success is a claim. Proof names the exact result, sources, conditions and separate checker; unknown is a real outcome (§5).
- Scripts own clocks, receipts, reservations, budgets and routing; useful progress differs from a live process; expired permission does not prove a process stopped (§§3,6,10).
- Silence creates no permission. Preferences are not authority; rules are scoped, conflicting rules stop the disputed action, irreversible effects retain approval (§§4,7,10).
- Trust reduces required human viewing, never evidence; it needs mature outcomes, independent sampling and his grant, and reverses after a miss (§7).
- Review capacity is minutes plus age, not seven items; direct controls, original evidence and protected dissent survive the single doorway; the meter includes failures and recovery (§§4,8).

## Forks that change the failure risk

- **Must the record survive a host loss without stopping?** R/C/O use one active writer with checked restoration; P's full design requires two of three keepers, although its pilot also uses one writer (§§2,10,13). One writer costs availability; majority storage costs operations, replicated attachments and partition recovery. Choose one writer and a tested backup first; add standard majority storage only if measured outage losses justify it. A sleeping second Mac is not automatically an independent failure domain.
- **Does every moved promise interrupt him?** R pushes a separate deduplicated promise-change notice (§4); C/O/P normally show it at next opening (§9). P also holds excess urgent cases behind a reserve, whereas O explicitly refuses to suppress a distinct genuine deadline (§4). Choose next-opening notice for non-actionable changes, O's exception for every independently verified time-critical consequence, and a separate infrastructure-failure alert. This saves interruptions but requires an honest dated glance and prompt detection; a notification budget must never become a veto on bad news.
- **What earns less viewing, and who is sampled?** O starts with seven-day outcomes; R/C/P use roughly two days; C changes human sampling in stages, R/O emphasize secondary checking, P books a random human sample before work (§7). Choose P's advance independent selection plus C's explicit graded permission, with the defect window tied to the actual work. Human and checker audits answer different questions and both cost capacity; three successes or a fixed percentage proves neither reliability nor taste.
- **Does preparing a showing require another helper?** C/O budget a showing writer; R/P render already checked facts (§§3,11). Choose direct rendering unless a new interpretation must be checked. This removes a fresh-start cost and a chance to introduce an unsupported claim; a complex explanation still needs examination.

## Failure trials: what catches it, when, at what cost, what he sees

### 1. A helper stops silently
- **R:** Progress lease trips within eleven minutes (§6); revoke its publishing right, preserve artifacts and reroute inside the two-attempt budget. Cost: minute scans plus replacement/checking. He sees nothing if the promise holds, otherwise one changed-promise notice; stopping the old process is still unproved.
- **C:** Dead-host renewal fails within eleven minutes; a live but stuck process trips the useful-progress clock by twenty-one (§§6,10). Cost: observation, bounded diagnosis/replacement and recheck. He sees nothing unless a consequence needs him or the promise changes at next opening.
- **O:** Missing renewal trips within six minutes, or no useful checkpoint within sixteen (§§6,9). Cost: inspect effects, fence, one retry and fresh examination. He sees nothing if recovery succeeds; otherwise a held goal or changed promise at next opening.
- **P:** No useful checkpoint trips within twelve minutes (§6); stale returns are fenced and a bounded chunk rerouted. Cost: packaging, another run and checks. He sees nothing if the promise holds. A twenty-minute claim alone would catch later; the progress clock matters.

### 2. A helper waits for something that will never come
- **R:** Named wait and deadline required; default outer return one workday, or eleven-minute stall catch for an undeclared wait (§6). Cost: one source observation, permitted fallback/park. He sees a changed promise or an honestly parked goal, never an instruction to chase the dependency himself.
- **C:** Explicit due event plus daily recheck catches it at deadline; an undeclared stall reaches the progress limit (§6). Cost: source query and alternative or park. He sees the current consequence at next opening, a prepared choice only if his authority is required.
- **O:** Wait lacking owner/event/fallback is rejected immediately; normally return by next working day (§§6,10). Cost: clerk query and authorized alternative or examination. He sees a hold/return, or one necessary choice; no live helper remains waiting.
- **P:** Deadline plus next two-minute sweep, external replies also checked daily with an initial two-working-day limit (§6). Cost: source verification and parked return checks. He sees the parked goal and revised promise. The longer policy permits an extra day's delay relative to R/O.

### 3. A message is lost, late, or read by the wrong part
- **R:** Durable pending work, same-receipt retries after thirty seconds and two-minute unclaimed-offer routing recover loss (§§2,6); version/role checks reject stale or unauthorized writes at commitment. Cost: reconciliation traffic. He normally sees nothing, or “not yet shared” during failure. A permitted recipient misunderstanding the instruction is **not caught** by a mandatory read-back.
- **C:** Reconcile after one minute and reuse the operation number, bounded retries (§6); role and version checks reject wrong-role/late publication (§§2,10). Cost: reads/retries or incident park. He sees unsaved intake/unknown or a consequence, not duplicate work. A right-role receiver misreading meaning is **not caught** by an explicit closed-loop receipt.
- **O:** Same-reference receipt retry after thirty seconds, unclaimed-order scan within two minutes, completion reconciliation each minute (§§6,10). Read-back exposes wrong understanding before effects (§2); source/version checks reject late answers. Cost: receipt and examination. He sees a material changed interpretation as a proposal; sender confirmation of every read-back is not fully specified.
- **P:** Next action survives in book/package and is reconstructed within two minutes; claims, versions and structured read-back reject stale/mismatched returns (§§2–3,6). Cost: reconciliation and packaging. He sees “not saved” for an uncommitted answer. Misaddressing that preserves a valid role/version but changes meaning has no guaranteed semantic catch.

### 4. A helper says “done” and it is not
- **R:** Maker cannot issue a pass; exact-result script and meaning checks block showing before exposure (§5). Cost: independent run, counterexample and repair. He sees only a checked showing or explicit uncertainty; omitted requirements remain **not caught** until challenge, audit or use, without a time bound.
- **C:** Protected check plan, independent reproduction and “could a useless result pass?” challenge act before work/showing (§5). Cost: early challenger, adversarial reviewer and seeded faulty control. He sees actual result with remaining human judgement; a shared omission is still **not caught** on a guaranteed schedule.
- **O:** Clerk cannot close on maker's word; scripts and examiner independently exercise result before showing (§5). Cost: examination, counterexample and periodic known-fault rehearsals. He sees checked criteria plus assumptions. Concealed/unobservable defects remain **not caught** until contrary evidence or use (§10).
- **P:** Role-limited pass rights, frozen plan and evidence bindings block the next step (§5). Cost: independent source check and realistic counterexample. He sees per-line verdicts and timestamps; a false criterion accepted by all remains **not caught** until audit, owner reaction or an escaped defect.

### 5. A helper restarts and has forgotten
- **R:** Fresh full-goal packet and current claim are required before writing (§§2–3,12). Cost: bounded reread and observing any uncertain effect, not a conversational handover. He sees no rediscovery question unless the needed fact was never recorded; that missing fact is unknown.
- **C:** New attempt reads exact agreement, sources and durable checkpoints; old permission cannot publish (§§3,10). Cost: fresh input and replay of incomplete checks. He sees nothing if facts suffice; missing evidence blocks continuation rather than becoming invented history.
- **O:** Current order, relevant instructions and recipient read-back precede effects (§§2–3,12). Cost: packet reread/examination on mismatch. He sees only a material proposal change. A forgotten unrecorded assumption is **not caught** merely by having a register.
- **P:** Contents, evidence, current rules and next action travel together; first write rejects old claim/version (§§2,12). Cost: attachment storage/transfer and reread. He sees no session recovery chore; absent credentials or hardware still parks work because portability is not capability.

### 6. Two helpers change the same thing
- **R:** Shared-resource reservation before dispatch, private copies and numbered publishing right reject stale commits immediately (§§3,6). Cost: serialization and integrated recheck. He sees no conflict unless it changes his tradeoff/promise. Uncontrolled external targets require known outcome before a second writer.
- **C:** Reservations, isolated candidates and atomic base-version comparison reject the loser at publication (§§3,10). Cost: discard/rebase work and rerun interface checks. He sees only an unresolved owner choice or promise change; a wrapper without actual write control supplies no protection.
- **O:** Reserve resources; one result commits against a base, with expiring effect credentials and reconciliation (§§6,10). Cost: waiting, conflict repair and re-examination. He sees a hold if it affects delivery. Mere submission expiry cannot prevent a worker with uncontrolled live credentials changing the target.
- **P:** Common-book reservation plus claim/base check prevents duplicate commitment; undeclared overlap faces integration checks (§§2–3,10). Cost: global coordination and possible discarded package work. He sees a delay only when consequential; an undeclared semantic overlap is **not caught** reliably by object locks.

### 7. A computer is off for a night
- **R:** Two missed minute availability renewals, caught within three minutes, then lease recovery (§6). Cost: reroute/recheck or wait for unique hardware. He gets one pushed Thursday-to-Friday line if the promise slips (§9); the record host needs separate treatment below.
- **C:** Host observation becomes unknown within three minutes; forecast reevaluates within five (§§6,10). Cost: replacement or machine-bound park. He sees the changed promise at next opening; only a verified urgent owner action interrupts him.
- **O:** Two missed minute probes, caught within three minutes; assess affected promises immediately (§6). Cost: rerouting or held local-only work and new examination. He sees Friday and the reason once on opening, earlier only for time-critical consequences.
- **P:** Source/host observation expires within ten minutes plus a two-minute sweep (§10). Cost: package transfer/reroute or park. He sees Friday at next opening. Full majority storage may survive one record keeper; its single-writer pilot stops if that writer was the sleeping computer (§13).

### 8. The record cannot be reached
- **R:** Failed commit stops effects immediately; separate probe marks unavailable within two minutes (§§6,10). Cost: safe stoppage, checked backup restore and target reconciliation; up to five minutes of backup loss is admitted. He sees dated unknown and “not yet shared,” never a fresh all-clear.
- **C:** Watch/doorway mark unknown within three minutes; no new authority/publication (§10). Cost: queued private work, fenced single-writer restoration and receipt reconciliation. He sees dated snapshot/unknown and incident delivery where available; simultaneous loss of observer and communication has no alert bound.
- **O:** Next minute clerk detects failure, second host can alert; total loss stops unverifiable effects (§§6,10). Cost: checked-copy restoration, writer-generation fencing and effect reconciliation. He sees unknown/service failure and unsent receipts; no private shadow register becomes authority.
- **P:** No majority blocks new claims/effects; last healthy probe expires by minute eight (§§2,6). Cost: reduced availability, replica repair and attached-proof verification; pilot lacks majority survival (§13). He can inspect a dated package offline, sees unknown, and cannot mistake private drafting for accepted shared progress.

### 9. The record is wrong or hours out of date
- **R:** Source/result versions invalidate dependent proof; live observation expires at ten minutes, checked on minute watch (§§2,5–6). Cost: independent reread/recheck; he sees unknown/stale instead of green. A freshly recorded fabricated pass or shared semantic mistake is **not caught** by ordering and hashes; no guaranteed discovery time.
- **C:** Proof binds exact inputs and check version; per-check expiry/follow-up plus daily overdue scan invalidates stale claims (§§5–6). Cost: reproduce and inspect; he sees unknown and withheld completion. There is no universal live-state freshness bound, and wrong-but-current source facts are **not caught** without a contrary observation.
- **O:** Observation time, next-due boundary and independent source examination distinguish reports from reality (§§2,5–6). Cost: source re-examination and fresh packet. He sees “not observed since …”. No universal source-freshness limit is specified; an examiner's confidently wrong attestation remains **not caught** until another check contradicts it.
- **P:** Fingerprints and two-copy presence protect package identity; ten-minute live freshness plus sweep bounds ordinary staleness (§§2,6). Cost: refresh/replication and recheck; he sees unknown and missing coverage. Two keepers agreeing on a false pass preserve the same falsehood: semantic record error is **not caught** by replication.

### 10. The watch itself has stopped
- **R:** Separate monitor detects missed probes or watch position older than two minutes, inhibits external effects and changes doorway (§6). Cost: second failure domain and incident recovery. He sees state unavailable plus one independently routed exception; if both observers fail, timely detection is **not caught** within a bound.
- **C:** Separately hosted observer detects three minutes without watch/record observation (§6). Cost: second host, probe, restart/incident delivery. He sees unknown and last confirmation. Whether probe proves the full clock-to-effect path still needs testing; correlated observer/communication failure is **not caught** on time (§10).
- **O:** Second host notices three-minute scan staleness within the next minute and attempts takeover (§6). Cost: independent service, checked takeover and possible alert. He sees stale observation/service failure; live-but-broken due processing is **not caught** by a fresh scan timestamp alone.
- **P:** Two independent clocks check each other's end-to-end write/read probes; missing success for six minutes is exposed by minute eight (§6). Cost: duplicate sweeps and recovery. He sees unknown, including offline expiry. A probe that works while real due actions are skipped is **not caught** unless a due-action canary is added.

### 11. The checker shares the maker's blind spot
- **R:** Original intent before maker's account, independent evidence, attempted counterexample and secondary random audits reduce the chance (§§5,7,14). Cost: separate review/audit; he sees assumptions and dissent. A shared wrong interpretation is **not caught** on any bound; a second identity alone does not restore independence.
- **C:** Separate contract challenge, maker-blind review, own edge case, seeded fault and consequential method/provider diversity are strongest here (§5). Cost: challenger, fault controls and scarce specialist. He sees unresolved human judgement or parked work. A blind spot absent from those controls is **not caught** until new evidence/use.
- **O:** Independent source path, counterexample, different method where practical, known-defect rehearsal and qualified checker for consequential work (§5). Cost: examination/specialist and audits. He sees unresolved assumptions rather than a combined score; a novel shared misconception remains **not caught** without outside evidence.
- **P:** Source-first checker, realistic counterexample, specialist/distinct model for consequential ambiguity, raw dissent and random audits (§5). Cost: independent investigation; he sees blocked completion or real tradeoff. Same-source/systematic errors can still be **not caught** indefinitely; portable proof does not make its interpretation correct.

### 12. A default acts without authority
- **R:** Agreement/rule/resource versions and effect service permission check reject it at action boundary; unaccepted goals cannot start by timeout (§§2,4). Cost: explicit grants and some parks. He sees authorized assumptions or safe stop. Misclassifying a preference as permission is contained only if these are genuinely separate enforceable fields.
- **C:** Rule authority is separate from preference; default requires delegated scope, tested undo and limit; sole publisher verifies permission (§§4,10). Cost: undo rehearsal/checks or park. He sees no unauthorized action, only a prepared reserved choice. An overbroad accepted grant can still authorize an unwanted effect.
- **O:** Standing order/default must fit explicit limits; clerk-controlled effects use expiring permission (§§4,10). Cost: examined interpretation and checks. He sees authorized default or held work, with receipt. “Restore” as a fallback must itself fit authority; calling it safe is not permission to destroy new data.
- **P:** Common authority version, role-limited action script and imported-rule candidate status reject unauthorized action (§§2,4,7). Cost: current-book read and occasional park. He sees assumption/park; copied permission in an offline package is insufficient. All protection fails if the runner retains unrestricted live credentials (§12).

### 13. The gate holds back something he needed to know
- **R:** Protected checker dissent, original evidence, direct controls and independent urgent test bypass the preparer (§4). Cost: independent necessity/urgency review. He sees eligible bad news or moved promise; a fact no checker records is **not caught**, and the bypass still shares gate/record infrastructure.
- **C:** Checker can file directly; original record is open; genuine urgency can produce combined incident beyond reserve (§§4–5). Cost: review and incident capacity. He sees a real exception or dated hold. A needed warning wrongly classified as nonurgent is **not caught** by the cap; park alone does not inform him.
- **O:** Examiner's dissent cannot be rejected for disagreeing; checker may invoke urgent lane; distinct deadlines cannot be suppressed for a target (§4). Cost: source access and exceptional interruptions. He sees verified loss/harm. Decisive omitted facts remain **not caught** until challenge/audit (§14).
- **P:** Direct checker-to-gate path and inspectable original capsule help (§4), but overlapping urgent cases beyond reserve take safe fallback. Cost: investigation and potentially missed opportunity. A second distinct urgent act can be **not caught** as an actionable owner item in time; this is the reserve policy's largest attention risk.

### 14. A rule is misapplied or rules conflict
- **R:** Exact scope/exclusions, cited versions and explicit precedence checked before action; ambiguity goes to bounded interpretation (§§4,7). Cost: rule review or parked disputed choice. He sees one genuine unresolved decision. Quiet semantic mismatch is **not caught** until evidence contradicts it; thirty-day review only bounds stale review dates.
- **C:** Deterministic shortlist, source conditions and current instruction precedence; unresolved explicit conflict safely parks (§7). Cost: interpretation and one prepared ruling. He sees disputed scope, not arbitrary “latest wins.” A plausible false match is **not caught** on a guaranteed schedule.
- **O:** Case instruction/narrow order/wider order/advice hierarchy; equal-authority conflict freezes only disputed effect (§7). Cost: examined applicability or owner choice. He sees the concrete conflict with safe hold. Wrongly classified scope remains **not caught** until source comparison, dissent or outcome exposes it.
- **P:** Rich scope, exceptions, prior mistake and considered/applied/enforced-rule receipts; refresh before commitment (§7). Cost: more metadata and interpretation. He sees an unresolved conflict only if reserved. Bad import cannot itself grant authority, but missing a relevant exception is **not caught** merely by recording the search.

### 15. Two goals need the same thing or undo each other
- **R:** Atomic resource/dependency/review reservation before dispatch; base-version checks and integrated-result checks (§3). Cost: serialization and recheck; he sees a priority choice only if not delegated. Opposing business outcomes on different objects are **not caught** reliably by the declared dependency graph.
- **C:** Resource and dependency reservations plus integration checks reject conflicting versions (§§3,5,10). Cost: waiting, repair and revalidation; he sees changed promise/priority fork. “Both individually pass but defeat the combined goal” is **not caught** without an explicit cross-goal criterion.
- **O:** Work orders name exclusive resources, dependency and release; clerk admits and integrates against current base (§§2–3,12). Cost: waiting and examined reconciliation. He sees a held goal or real priority decision. Different files implementing incompatible policies are **not caught** by exclusive file ownership.
- **P:** Common book atomically reserves shared resources and human minutes; discovered dependency stops admission (§§2–4). Cost: global coordination, package refresh/integration. He sees parked conflict. Two undeclared effects that cancel each other are **not caught** just because each capsule is complete internally.

### 16. He answers nothing for five days
- **R:** Defaults act only within grants; unsafe choices/sign-in park; one-workday ready-age guard closes admission (§§4,10). Cost: delay, periodic script returns and lost release window. He sees current unknowns/consequences plus finite sitting; five days of silence never counts as acceptance.
- **C:** Default clocks and two-working-day age guard park owner branches and stop more starts (§§4,10). Cost: delayed scope, explicit slips and script revalidation. He sees dated truth and at most twelve estimated minutes initially; full backlog resolution is not promised.
- **O:** Return file, expiry and two-working-day capacity tradeoff hold owner work (§§4,10). Cost: lost deadlines and held throughput, not a waiting helper. He sees uncontained harm/missed promises first, then finite book; silence does not manufacture a priority ruling.
- **P:** Default/expiry and twenty-four-hour ready guard park goals; capacity prevents new review debt (§§4,10). Cost: missed sign-in, parked work and dated checks. He sees current consequences and a fifteen-minute target sitting; an expired item remains an obligation with a return, not erased success.

### 17. Ten goals become forty
- **R:** Future review minutes, resource reservations, first-ready age and merged jobs cap admission (§§3–4). Cost: lower throughput and parked dates; he sees explicit holds/estimates, not forty new questions. True owner-only demand above capacity is **not solved** by scheduling; priority/scope must change.
- **C:** Twelve-minute reservation plus queue age holds branches before more work starts (§4). Cost: estimates, parked goals and possibly one capacity ruling. He sees a finite sitting with honest backlog age. Underestimated minutes still create overload until measured service corrects admission.
- **O:** Book reserves next two sittings, holds excess goals and prepares capacity tradeoff (§4). Cost: preparation itself can bottleneck (§11); he sees explicit holds and needed priority choice. Forty valid matters cannot be made harmless by calling their papers expired.
- **P:** Common global reservations, twenty-four-hour ready age and separate cost limits stop hidden question growth (§4). Cost: central coordination and parked capacity checks. He sees finite sitting plus held goals; twenty-percent human sampling consumes real slots, and estimate errors must tighten admission rather than disappear.

### 18. An accepted result proves wrong after others build on it
- **R:** Defect reopens original goal, suspends rule/trust and finds dependent results on receipt (§§7,9). Cost: impact checks, authorized repair and independent restoration proof. He sees checked consequence/repair, urgently for continuing harm. Complete transitive withdrawal of downstream releases is **not specified**, so damage may continue beyond direct links.
- **C:** Escaped-defect event suspends autonomy/rule and traces related results immediately (§7). Cost: reproduction, bounded repair/rollback and recheck. He sees consequence or reserved remedy choice, with original acceptance preserved. No full downstream quarantine/release sequence is specified; indirect reliance is **not caught** unless traced.
- **O:** Defect freezes shortcut, rechecks work since last sound audit and reverses suspect rule (§7). Cost: potentially broad re-examination and remedy; he sees defect and remedy, new question only for new authority. Audit-window scope can miss older indirect dependents; a complete reliance map is **not specified**.
- **P:** Reopen capsule, revoke delegation, find rule applications and affected open/already-applied cases (§§6–7). Cost: investigations, package updates and checked repair. He sees impact/restoration choice if required and corrected ledger. Self-contained goals do not supply a complete cross-goal chain of downstream reliance automatically.

### 19. An irreversible action is about to execute on a wrong assumption
- **R:** Sole effect service verifies current authority, exact object versions, operation identity and uncertain prior outcomes before acting (§§2,6,12). Cost: preflight observation/serialization; absent approval, he sees a concrete reserved act. An authorized but false business assumption is **not caught** by these checks alone; no complete last-moment recipient/amount/audience read-back is specified.
- **C:** Irreversible work retains owner gate and independent methods; publisher checks permission and version (§§5,7,10). Cost: exact-result preparation, approval and final check. He sees unresolved assumption/authority. Approval can still be stale in meaning: a complete fresh assumption-and-target pause immediately before execution is **not specified**.
- **O:** Examined instruction, receiver read-back, explicit irreversible approval and one-use effect reference precede execution (§§2,7,10). Cost: examination, read-back and final observation. He sees the concrete approval question. An earlier read-back is not a last-second pause; changed amount/audience/business premise can remain **not caught** without a dedicated preflight.
- **P:** Intention and stable action key recorded first; current claim/authority checked; unknown effects reconciled before retry (§§7,10). Cost: preflight state reads, approval and no automatic retry where unsafe. He sees exact result/assumptions, but package integrity does not validate the business premise; a fresh irreversible-boundary read-back is **not specified**.

### 20. He stops believing “nothing needs you”
- **R:** Most trust-destroying failure: a false current-looking all-clear while an observer or authority source is stale. Freshness/independent monitor should expose it within two minutes (§6); semantic falsehood has no bound. Win belief back through a visible incident chain, demonstrated stale-to-unknown test, repaired evidence and full showing for affected scope; cost is recovery plus renewed review, not reassurance.
- **C:** Most trust-destroying failure: all lines green for the wrong interpretation (§14). No guaranteed catch; source-first challenge, two-day follow-up and audit may find it (§§5–7). Win belief back by showing the omitted requirement, a control that now fails bad work, real repaired outcome and temporary full review; he sees bounded claims rather than a completeness badge.
- **O:** Most trust-destroying failure: polished briefing omits the decisive bad news (§14). Dissent/audit may catch it, without a bound. Win belief back by exposing original paper and omission, independently rehearsing its urgent bypass and showing the corrected consequence before ordinary briefing; cost is examination, some extra interruptions and sampled original-source review.
- **P:** Most trust-destroying failure: every capsule looks sound while the common book, cross-goal effect or urgent filter is wrong (§§4,14). Freshness catches some cases within eight/twelve minutes; semantic inconsistency has no bound. Win belief back with a reconciled all-goal view, a demonstrated cross-goal failure case and direct evidence/urgent bypass; cost is impact repair, common-book operations and renewed human samples.

**Protections every choice needs:** fresh dated observations and an independent, exercised watch; exact-version authority and proof; enforced single publication rights; loss/late/duplicate receipt reconciliation; waits with owners, deadlines and return checks; safe parking; independent adversarial checks with faulty controls; contestable filtering and urgent dissent; scoped revocation with downstream impact review; stop/restore that states irreversible loss; admission by real review capacity; and a meter that includes mistakes, recovery and escaped defects. Never equate “no current action for you” with “everything is correct.”

## What the evidence demands that none fully supplies

- **A final irreversible-action procedure:** reobserve the target and consequential assumptions; bind exact artifact, audience/recipient/amount, authority and expiry; receiver reads back, sender confirms; then execute once and observe. All four have pieces, none specifies that complete boundary. `d7-other-fields.md` §§3,9,12 and `s-kinds-of-work.md` “Money, messages, public release” demand it; refunds/rollback cannot retract exposure.
- **A complete downstream-defect procedure:** record which accepted versions other results rely on, stop affected releases in dependency order, find already exposed recipients/targets, and check compensation separately from repair. All mention impact tracing; none defines closure and release of the whole affected chain. `d5-outside.md` §2 and `s-kinds-of-work.md` undo limits show why repairing the originating artifact is insufficient.
- **One common counting rule:** R counts presentation for reaction; C emphasizes requested answers; O excludes unviewed papers; P counts required state-settling responses (§8). ## Combined design — thirteen decisions

1. Use R's single ordered authority and rebuildable current views; one writer, checked backup and separate health observer first.
2. Give each job P's portable bounded contents, complete agreement and exact required proof; keep credentials outside it.
3. Preserve original words; use O's short instruction/read-back and C's independent challenge of meaning before expensive work.
4. Admit only named dependencies/resources and future human minutes; integrate against current versions, not separately green pieces.
5. Use C's source-first adversarial checker, protected checks and planted bad controls; require appropriate specialist/device/account proof.
6. Start with R's meaningful-progress clock and bounded recovery; measure false stalls before tightening it. Fence writes, then reconcile effects.
7. Add a due-action canary; the outside observer must verify the scheduler actually advances real due work and the doorway expires offline.
8. Rules are case rulings first; adopt P's receipts for rules considered, selected and enforced. Preference never grants permission.
9. Gate by measured minutes/age; moved promises wait for opening unless action is time-critical. Preserve O's route for every distinct real emergency.
10. Pause immediately before irreversible action with exact target/assumptions, current authority and confirmed read-back; observe outcome before retry.
11. Keep an explicit versioned reliance chain; a later defect quarantines affected outputs and released effects, with separately authorized remedies.
12. Trust changes exposure only after explicit scoped permission, relevant follow-up and independent preselected samples; no automatic three-pass promotion.
13. Render checked showings directly; retain sources, dissent and stop controls. Freeze raw cycle accounting; meter full cost, review/recovery minutes and defects.

**Smallest useful first version:** one reversible report or wording goal, one agreed outcome, one isolated maker, one separate checker, direct artifact showing, a manually authorized narrow rule, timed return/park, one retry, one writer and outside probe. No generalized rule inference, automatic promotion, majority storage or uncontrolled live effects. Include raw meter receipts before the first goal; use a second computer only for the recovery exercise initially.
**The win to prove:** a failed check returns directly to repair and a genuinely covered fork uses the existing grant, eliminating an owner check-in/repeated decision while preserving agreement plus showing. Compare the same bounded goal with today's metered path; fewer avoidable cycles and owner minutes, no extra escaped defect, and affordable total recovery cost are the acceptance test. Historical records do not yet prove that win.

## Cheap tests required before building the product

- **Run this matrix as executable paper trials:** fake clock and in-memory state; inject every failure into each design. Assert who detects it, deadline, allowed next effect and exact visible sentence. Missing transitions remain “not caught”; a successful prose trace is not an integration test.
- **Kill/partition/late-return trial:** disposable local files and two mock runners; kill maker/watch, lose an acknowledgement, replay old output, partition writer and restore backup. Assert one authorized commit, no blind external retry and automatic unknown display; count recovery work.
- **Observer test:** let the health probe succeed while due jobs deliberately never run; the canary must fail. Stop both local observers and disconnect the doorway; verify cached all-clear expires. No phone message needs sending to test policy logic.
- **Blind checker trial:** hide maker's explanation; plant wrong requirement, plausible false pass, missing device proof and shared bad source among clean candidates. Measure catches, false objections and human review cost; a green test suite that accepts the planted failure disqualifies the check.
- **Authority/release rehearsal:** fake money/message target; change recipient/amount/authority after approval, lose execution reply, and replay a queued phone answer. Require final read-back, current versions, no duplicate and a safe stop; test dissent bypass with two simultaneous real deadlines.
- **Cross-goal/late-defect rehearsal:** one goal consumes an accepted output, another changes a different object to undo it; discover source defect after both close. Require full affected-chain discovery, quarantine, authorized repair and fresh integrated proof, preserving original meter totals.
- **Forty-goal/five-day replay:** use current withdrawn/set-aside distinctions, recorded question/answer examples and a fixed review-minute budget. Count holds, missed opportunities, words and return work; verify no vanished obligations or assumed consent. Blind agreement writers to later answers, then compare actual corrections.
- **One measured real pilot after those pass:** existing reversible work only; current workflow versus proposed path with identical checks and frozen counting. Record setup, actual/unknown charges, escaped defects and recovery minutes; only use can establish sustained trust, attention load and phone delivery, so do not claim those from replay.

**Ranking for this lens:** R first for explicit authority, freshness and effect recovery; C second and strongest on false proof; O third for clear read-back and dissent but higher paper cost; P fourth because mature replication adds failure modes and its urgent reserve can suppress a distinct needed act. P's portable packet and rule-use receipts belong in the combined version despite that ranking.
