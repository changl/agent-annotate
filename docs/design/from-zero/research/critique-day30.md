## After the demonstration

Every stress below states the likely screen, the first break, the coping behavior, and what would let it survive. None of the prototypes actually simulates forty goals or five days of arbitrary missed sittings; these are extrapolations, not hidden implemented states.

**Thread**
- **Forty goals / four projects:** the current sheet remains small while In hand becomes a long drawer/right rail and Settled gains days; competing work loses visibility first. He asks “what else is waiting?” and checks goal replies. It needs a bounded cross-goal exception summary and retrieval by goal/ruling, with all unserved work accounted for.
- **Bad day: three stalls, seventeen questions, urgent release, accepted miss:** the drawing can put the miss first, show the urgent knock, and fold notices into history, but it offers no rule for simultaneous ordering. He skims the top, presses the recommended batch, then checks the raw record when something surprises him. Keep one incident summary separating repaired stalls, slipped commitments, unknown work, and his one urgent act; never let answering one item silently acknowledge every notice.
- **Five days away:** expect the same three-item welcome sentence and a longer “while away” block above it; bounded recovery is not designed. At 1280×900 even the existing four catch-up lines leave almost no result visible above the persistent Accept controls. He starts accepting the summary. Move catch-up into a short consequence/authority digest with an explicit remaining-review budget and resumable split.
- **Most sittings skipped:** authorized tool/layout defaults proceed and other goals park, but `hand()` treats earlier scripted moments as settled; the demo cannot establish that a missed owner answer survives. He checks the past or keeps a separate list. A real current view must derive pending obligations from actual answers, not elapsed story moments, and report capacity lost through absence.
- **Day 30, wrong rules, uneven trust, long history:** receipts multiply, generic “always” rulings become invisible assumptions, and a money miss narrows trust without showing all decisions made under the mistaken ruling. He repeats instructions and retracts broad freedoms. Show a disputed ruling's exact source, scope, affected current work, and a way to suspend that scope while checking consequences; avoid making him read a rule encyclopedia.
- **Real question mix / hard correction / disbelief:** prose and price pairs work; a hidden sign-up behavior, instruction conflict, or real release is not just a point on a sheet. “Try it on your own phone” supplies no functioning return channel for a mark. He reverts to chat and screenshots. It needs revision-bound behavior evidence, instruction before/after, and an explicit audience/effect for an acceptance that also releases.

**Sitting**
- **Forty goals / four projects:** the door can still present a finite session while Everything moving grows underneath. What is held out of the session becomes the first uncertainty. He opens the glance repeatedly. Preserve the bounded session, but show oldest unserved age, total review minutes, urgent reserve, and why the omitted work can safely wait; no forty-item daily scan.
- **Bad day:** the miss, three recovered/slipped stalls, and three owner forks compete for the running order; the watcher and sign-in notification have distinct screens, but there is no combined bad-day state. He skips to easy items or clears warning lines with Begin. Let the independent observer prepare one consequence-based recovery order, leaving repaired stalls as optional evidence and protecting time for the urgent identity action.
- **Five days away:** six things/sixteen minutes already offers “first three now” and a scheduled remainder, the most honest seed here. Five days could mean forty minutes, not sixteen. He can stop without treating the rest as forgotten. Keep the split, revalidate stale evidence and defaults before offering the next slice, and make the oldest consequential wait visible after stopping.
- **Most sittings skipped:** scheduled remainders become appointments he also misses; the green end screen risks feeling like finishing when it is postponing. He ignores the sitting habit or starts skimming Accept. Recompute admission from observed attendance, park new work before it produces more human review, and make “not this week” a bounded authority/capacity instruction rather than a task on every goal.
- **Day 30:** random trusted samples, permission proposals, rule corrections, and the weekly page join the same human queue. Review becomes the backlog, precisely D6's failure. He fast-forwards sample results or skips the ledger. Budget audits by risk and uncertainty, keep technical rechecks with staff, and show only changed/revoked authority plus affected work to him.
- **Real mix / disbelief:** price comparison and editable agreement lines are practical; sequential focus cannot compare two related goals, a shared instruction, or a current versus earlier layout. He opens extra windows and writes a long correction. Keep the finite session while allowing a related-evidence comparison inside an item, a behavior trace, and original intent beside the corrected instruction; the checker route must return a real answer.

**Picture**
- **Forty goals / four projects:** ten rows per project make several phone screens and a tall desk contents page; red flags and fixed positions no longer provide one glance. Spatial overview breaks before the individual card. He scans every section, misses lower red marks, then ignores quiet rows or keeps another shortlist. Keep stable identity/location in depth, but make the front page a bounded set of changed consequences and the next human commitment; expose omissions and oldest age.

Partial uncertainty is excellent, but it needs one grouped incident/impact summary and a prepared order so red does not mean “find out what happened yourself.”

He ignores the red or approves a batch to restore the clean page. Report which work was withheld/parked by capacity, make return plans explicit, and never make removing red the reason to accept.

## Craft and what to steal

- **Thread:** readable warm paper/light and dark, understandable words, useful keyboard access; however, large greetings, old receipts, two desk sidebars, and fixed answer controls compete with the actual thing. On the return desktop screenshot, the artifact is a thin strip while Accept remains prominent. The prototype toolbar exaggerates this, but removing it does not remove the growing catch-up block. It resembles a three-pane chat client despite folding its transcript. Steal direct line marks and a receipt saying exactly what was agreed, not the permanently visible history.
- **Sitting:** clearest hierarchy and most legible phone controls in both themes; desk result/checks/order columns make the relationship understandable. Long agreement/weekly pages still permit Go/Done before all content has been read, and correction adds a mode. It resembles a review wizard more than a task manager; that is useful until the appointment becomes a queue-clearing ritual. Steal the running order, minutes, and real choice to end a partial session.
- **Picture:** calm book-like contents and facing page in both themes; hatching plus words communicates uncertainty without relying only on red. The nine-goal phone screen is already nearly full before opening anything, and an opened result pushes the picture away. Forty rows bring back the project board through typography. Steal stable goal homes, local notices, and partial unknown states; reserve the complete contents for deliberate retrieval.
- **Letter:** convincing paper/enclosure distinction; blue marks show whose judgment it is; pins and cuts remain readable in dark mode as well. Phone switching between letter and enclosure adds navigation, while the desk can show both. Dashed versus solid recommendation circles need their explanatory words; they cannot bear the authority distinction alone. It looks less like work tracking, but its glance is still a small application. Steal the enclosure, revocable default at its use, and unedited checker doubt beside the recommendation.

## What I would build, and what the method must change

Build Sitting's bounded, resumable encounter around Picture's stable goal identities; use Letter's markable enclosures, revocable defaults and checker dissent; keep Thread's direct corrections and concise answer receipts. The standing view should answer “what changed, what is consequentially unknown, what needs me now, what will safely wait?” with drill-down to all goals. Neither a permanent forty-row board nor a disappearing current turn supplies that answer by itself.

The missing object is an inspectable change in authority: the exact case/ruling, where it applies, what it permitted, what used it, and which outcomes must be revisited when it was wrong. Show the affected consequence when needed; do not make him maintain a policy dashboard. Trust is scoped permission plus relevant outcome evidence, not a count of accepted screens or a broad “wording” category. [Kinds of work](s-kinds-of-work.md) makes clear why a harmless local wording fix, a customer email, an instruction edit, and a public release need different evidence and authority.

The method's absolutes fail under these drawings: “never writes a specification” excludes the new requirements in D2; “decide once” needs context, supersession and expiry; “silence means all is well” must admit unobserved work; “nothing waits for ever” cannot manufacture an unavailable owner's consent; “cycles above par are waste” punishes useful discovery. A one-minute agreement cannot settle all behavior, security and business policy. One doorway must not imply one unchallengeable witness. These are corrections to the method, not excuses to add more approval screens.

Most importantly, a cap on visible minutes does not prove a cap on committed review. Keeping the next letter short can simply hide a queue. A skipped week must change what starts, what is parked, what promises slip, and how recovery is presented; the independent record must distinguish reported, observed, checked and accepted revisions. All four narrate parts of that machinery. None demonstrates that it holds without the person checking the helpers, checking the checker, and finally approving without reading.
