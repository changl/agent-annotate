# The test week

Every design concept and every workflow option is run against this same week, so they can be compared. The numbers are of the size the measurements show today. The goal names are examples.

## The person

One builder. Many helpers work for him on four projects, on three computers. He uses a phone and a desk. He wants plain words: no ids, no helper names, no jargon. His attention is the only scarce thing.

## What is going on (Tuesday morning)

| Goal, in his words | Project | Where it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faster sign-up | Driving app | Agreed yesterday, being built | Shows Thursday. Cycle 1, par 2. |
| Invoice export | Billing tool | Built and checked, ready to show | 6 "done" lines, all pass. 2 assumptions made. |
| Price page | Billing tool | A fork only he can decide | Yearly price first, or monthly? Default: yearly, at 5 pm. |
| Driver ratings | Driving app | Said last night by voice, agreement ready | Needs a sketch first (a decision of taste). Par 3. |
| Fix wrong totals on the weekly report | Billing tool | Done without him | Trusted kind of work. Par 0. Reported in the ledger. |
| Move photos to cheaper storage | Driving app | Being checked | Shows tomorrow. |
| New welcome email | Shop site | Sketch ready to show | Taste. Cycle 2, par 3. |
| Speed up the test run | Tools | Being built | Shows Friday. |
| Sales tax for two new states | Shop site | Parked until the accountant replies | He said "not now" last week. |

Behind this: 27 helpers on 3 computers. One computer is asleep.

## Events the design must carry

1. **The morning sitting.** Three things need him: agree (Driver ratings), look (Invoice export), decide (Price page). About eight minutes. Then nothing needs him.
2. **Saying it on the move.** At 12:40, walking, he says: "The reminder texts go out too late, people miss their lesson." No project chosen, no form.
3. **A flood.** In one afternoon the Driving app's helpers raise 17 questions. Earlier rulings answer 11. 3 can be undone, so they go ahead on the recommendation. 3 reach him.
4. **A send-back.** The welcome email sketch is wrong: too long, wrong tone. He points at what is wrong. He is asked once: here only, or always?
5. **Not now.** He parks "Driver ratings" until the sign-up work is done.
6. **A stall he never sees.** A helper goes silent for 40 minutes. Something else notices and restarts the work. The promised time holds.
7. **A stall he must see.** A computer is off all night. "Shows Thursday" becomes "shows Friday". He is told once, in one line, with the reason.
8. **Something that cannot wait for a sitting.** A release needs his sign-in before 3 pm or it slips a week. How does it reach him, and how is that kept rare?
9. **A question that died.** A question from last week no longer matters because the work moved on. It is withdrawn. He is told, not asked.
10. **Trust earned.** Three first-time acceptances in a row for "wording changes". The counterpart proposes: from now on these are done and reported, not shown.
11. **Trust lost.** A result he accepted turns out wrong two days later.
12. **Back after three days away.** What does he see first? How long until he is current?
13. **The week's ledger.** Goals done, cycles against par, minutes of his time, what was decided for him, rules learned, what was sent back and why, questions asked this week and last.
14. **Curiosity.** He wants to know who is working where, and what one piece of work has cost. One level down, never pushed.

## What is counted for each concept

For the whole week, for the person only:

- places he must look;
- times he is interrupted (anything he did not choose to open);
- things he must hold in his head between sittings;
- words he must read, and words he must type;
- taps or keys;
- decisions he makes, and how many of them only he could make;
- minutes.
