# The programme: from the method to a design we can build

12 Nov 2026. The method is in `../from-zero-20261112.md`. This file is the plan for proving and drawing it: what we need to know, where each answer comes from, who is finding it, and how the options are judged.

## 1. No week of watching

The first plan was to run the method by hand for a week before building. Chang's challenge: the record of how work went already exists, so what would a week buy? Answer: one thing only.

| What we need to know | Where the answer is | When we can have it |
|---|---|---|
| Where his attention goes today, per goal | 62 GB of transcripts, the review pages, the boards | Now. Workers D1, D2, D4. |
| Which questions truly needed him | The same | Now. D2. |
| What rules he has already given, and whether writing them down stopped the repeats | Memory notes, instruction files, his later messages | Now. D3. |
| How work stalls, and who notices | Transcripts and run records | Now. D1, then wave 2. |
| Whether the ten principles hold up outside our own reasoning | Published evidence and practice | Now. D5. |
| Whether an agreement up front would have prevented what went wrong later | Replay: write the agreement from his first sentence on past goals, blind, then compare with what happened | Wave 2. |
| Whether he recognises the agreement, the sitting and the glance as right | His reaction to his own past goals replayed, and to the design concepts | One sitting of about 20 minutes. |
| Whether silence stays believable, whether the asking falls week on week, whether trust is earned | Only time in real use | Cannot be had sooner. Not a reason to wait: it is measured from the first day of use. |

So the week is dropped. What replaces it: mine the record, replay the method against it, show him the result, and build the measuring into the first thing that is built.

## 2. One correction to the method

Chang's point: one helper that runs for a month, or helpers handing over to each other, will not give clear progress or completion. That is right, and the method must say so plainly.

- **The counterpart is a role, not a session.** Each job it does (write an agreement, prepare a question, prepare a showing, write the ledger) is a short run that starts from the record and ends. Nothing needs to stay alive.
- **The record is the only memory.** Any helper can pick up any goal cold. There are no hand-overs because there is nothing to hand over.
- **Progress is read from the record, not reported.** A goal's progress is how many of its "done" lines pass their checks. Completion is all of them passing and his acceptance.

## 3. Core components

Behind the screen, nine parts. Each exists to close a gap or to protect his attention.

| Part | What it does | Closes |
|---|---|---|
| The record | Holds every goal, agreement, "done" line, ruling, rule, result and cycle. The only memory. | Sight |
| The counterpart | Short runs that turn a sentence into an agreement, a fork into a prepared question, finished work into a showing. | Intent |
| The rule book | His rulings and preferences. Looked up before any question may reach him. Each rule carries the case it came from and how often it was applied or overturned. | Intent |
| The gate | The only way to his attention. In order: rule book, default with a time, the cap, expiry, then the next sitting. One narrow lane for what cannot wait. | Attention |
| The doers | Work from an agreement, in silence. Any kind of helper on any machine. They never reach him. | |
| The checks | Every "done" line is tied to a check. Checks with no judgement in them run as scripts. What needs judgement is checked by a different helper than the maker. The proof is kept. | Trust |
| The watch | A clock on everything in flight: promised times, stalls, expiry. A script, not a helper. It restarts or reroutes. He sees a change only if a promise moves. | Sight |
| The trust table | For each kind of work: first-time acceptances and misses. Sets par: show everything, show a sample, or do and report. | Trust |
| The meter | Counts cycles and their causes, his minutes, his words, and the cost of each goal. Built in from the first day. | |

On the screen, three surfaces carry the five moments:

- **Say.** One line, typed or spoken, from anywhere.
- **Sit.** A short, finite stack of prepared things: agree, look, decide. "Look" opens the thing itself, and a correction is made by pointing at it.
- **Glance.** One line of truth, the goals with the time each will next be shown, the week's ledger. The engine room is one level down.

And one rare push, for what cannot wait for a sitting.

Whether these three are one place, three places or no new place at all is the open design question. Four designers are answering it separately (section 4).

## 4. The work

**Wave 1, running now.**

| Who | What | Result |
|---|---|---|
| D1, GPT 6.1 Sol | Script that pulls every message he typed out of all transcripts on three Macs, with what he was answering and what he got back | A corpus later workers can read cheaply |
| D2, GPT 6.1 Sol | Every question put to him on a review page: what he chose, typed, how long it waited, whether it needed him | Rows and shares |
| D3, GPT 6.1 Sol | Every standing instruction he has given, and whether each one stopped the repeat | The seed rule book |
| D4, GPT 6.1 Sol | How goals travelled through boards, worker runs and hand-overs | Baselines, and the share of work spent on the working system itself |
| D5, GPT 6.1 Sol | Outside evidence for and against each principle; patterns and traps from other fields and products | Verdict per principle |
| Four designers, two Fable and two Opus | Four separate concepts, each a clickable prototype of the same test week (`scenario.md`), phone and desk, light and dark | `concepts/thread`, `sitting`, `picture`, `letter` |

The four premises are deliberately far apart: one conversation; a finite stack worked through at a sitting; one true picture; no app at all (a briefing paper and a reply). Each designer is told how their premise usually fails.

**Wave 2, starts when D1's corpus lands.**

| What | How | Judged by |
|---|---|---|
| What each of his messages was | Workers label every message he typed: new goal, correcting a misunderstanding, a decision of taste, an answer that needed him, an answer that did not, a status check, a standing rule, a nudge after a stall, a change of mind. Also: could the helper already have known? | A blind second label on a sample |
| Cycles per goal today | Rebuild about 60 goals across four projects from first sentence to acceptance. Count the cycles and give each one cause. | This is the baseline for the headline number |
| How work stalls | Count stalls, false "done", lost messages, memory lost at a restart; how long each lasted; who noticed, him or the system | Sets what the watch and the checks must catch |
| Replay | For about 30 past goals, three ways of writing the agreement (his sentence alone; with the rule book; with the rule book and a look at the project first), written blind. Then compare with what happened. | A different model scores which later cycles each agreement would have prevented |
| The machine, in options | For the record, the gate, the checks and the watch: two or three designs each, written separately by GPT 6.1 Sol and Opus | The test week and the stall census |
| The concepts, walked | Two critics, one GPT 6.1 Sol and one Claude, blind to each other, walk every concept through the test week and count what it costs him | The counts at the end of `scenario.md` |

**Wave 3.** One design: the screens combined from the best of the four, the machine chosen from the options, a second design round on real cases from the corpus, and a build plan in stages with the meter in the first stage.

## 5. How options are judged

In this order:

1. Cycles to done against par, on replayed goals.
2. What the test week costs him: places to look, interruptions, things held in his head, words read and typed, minutes.
3. Whether silence is believable: every stall in the census must be caught by something other than him.
4. Cost per goal, against today.
5. Quality: every "done" line checked by something other than the maker.

## 6. What reaches Chang

Nothing until the showing. Then, in one sitting of about 20 minutes:

- the four concepts to click through, with the critics' counts and a recommendation;
- five of his own past goals replayed: the agreement the method would have given him on day one, and what it would have saved;
- the findings, one page;
- the decisions only he can make. Expected: two or three, mostly taste.

Par for this programme from here: 2 cycles. The showing, then the build plan.

## 7. Limits kept

- At most ten workers at once on M1 Max. One heavy process each, at low priority.
- Other Macs are read over SSH only. Nothing is written or installed on them.
- Raw text from transcripts stays out of git, in a local folder. Only numbers and paraphrase are committed.
- Workers change nothing: no pages, boards, schedules, skills or instruction files.
- Designers may not open the old tool or the first attempt.
