# Getting work done through others, from zero

A restart, 12 Nov 2026. The first attempt (now in `first-attempt-20261112/`) improved an existing tool. That was the wrong job. This document starts from the problem and names no tool until its last section. The first attempt is kept only for its measurements.

## 1. The problem

One person wants more things to exist than they can make alone. Other hands are plentiful. The person's attention is not.

So the question is not how to manage work. It is: **how little of one person's attention can it take to turn what they want into a finished, correct result made by others?**

## 2. Where the attention goes

When work passes through another mind, three gaps open. All the effort of directing work is spent crossing them.

| Gap | The question in the person's head | How it is usually crossed |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Do they know what I want? | Briefs, specifications, long answers, corrections |
| Sight | What is happening, and what was actually done? | Status reports, checking in, reading |
| Trust | Will they do what I would have done when I am not looking? | Reviews, approvals, checking again |

Two more costs come from being one mind:

- Every unfinished thing the person must remember keeps part of their mind busy until it is closed, or held somewhere they trust.
- Every interruption costs the time it takes to rebuild what it was about.

A perfect helper, a copy of you with your judgement and full honesty, would need one sentence and return a finished result. All three gaps would be zero. The aim is to get as near to that as real helpers allow, and nearer every week.

## 3. Principles

None is new. Each is how people already direct work where attention is short and mistakes are dear.

| | Principle | Where it is already proven | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Attention is the only scarce thing. Spend everything else to save it.** | A factory line moves at the pace of its slowest station, so everything is arranged around that station. | Try and show, rather than ask. Check before showing. Prepare every decision fully. Nothing unprepared or unchecked reaches the person. |
| 2 | **People find out what they want by reacting, not by specifying.** | Architects show a sketch before a drawing, and a drawing before concrete. | The person never writes a specification. They are shown the cheapest thing that can be reacted to: the goal said back, then what "done" means, then a sketch, then a first slice. |
| 3 | **A misunderstanding costs more the later it is found. Close the intent gap before the work.** | An officer says the order back, in their own words, before moving. | One agreement before work starts. It is the most valuable minute the person spends. |
| 4 | **Decide once.** | A court's ruling becomes precedent. | Every answer is also a rule about what the person prefers. The rule is kept and that kind of question is not asked again. A working system asks less every week. |
| 5 | **Silence means all is well, and must be believable.** | In a cockpit, a dark panel means everything is normal. | No status. No news that needs nothing. One glance shows the truth whenever the person chooses to look. A stall is caught by something other than the one who stalled. The first time silence lies, the person goes back to checking and the load returns. |
| 6 | **Nothing waits on the person silently, and nothing waits for ever.** | A good assistant says: "Unless you tell me otherwise, I will do this on Friday." | Every question says what happens if it is not answered, and when. |
| 7 | **Show the thing, not a report about it. Check it before it is shown, and not by the one who made it.** | A bank's maker is never its checker. A builder walks the client through the house, not through a report on the house. | The person looks only at what only a person can judge, on the thing itself, and corrects by pointing at what is wrong. |
| 8 | **One counterpart, one doorway.** | An executive deals with one chief of staff, not with every team. | One place to say what you want. One place where what needs you appears. How many helpers, projects and machines stand behind it is not the person's concern unless they ask. |
| 9 | **Trust is earned for each kind of work, and buys freedom to proceed.** | A new hire's work is all reviewed; a veteran's is sampled. | Each first-time acceptance widens what goes ahead without the person. A miss narrows it. This is what makes the load keep falling instead of falling once. |
| 10 | **Start no more than the person can finish.** | A kitchen does not cook dishes that nobody can carry out. | Results waiting to be looked at and questions waiting to be answered go stale. New work starts at the rate the person closes what reaches them. |

Effort is not free. But the largest waste of effort is work built on a misunderstanding, so principles 2, 3 and 7 save effort as well as attention.

## 4. The method

One loop for each goal. The person is in it twice.

```text
say it ──► agreement ──► silence ──► showing ──► done
 you        you, about a minute        you: accept, or point at what is wrong
                │
                └─ a question reaches you only if a fork appears that only you can decide
```

| Step | What happens | The person's part |
|---|---|---|
| Say it | A goal in any form: a sentence, a voice note, "like that one, but…". | Seconds |
| Agreement | It comes back as: what I heard, what done means, what I will decide myself, what only you can decide, what I am not doing, when you will next hear from me. | Read, fix a line or say go |
| Silence | The work is done. Forks are settled by the agreement, by earlier rulings, or by a stated assumption. | Nothing |
| Showing | The result itself, every "done" line already checked, the assumptions listed. | Accept, or point at what is wrong. A correction is asked once: "here only, or always?" |
| Done | The ruling and the outcome go into the record. | Nothing |

**Who does what.** The person. One counterpart, who knows the person's preferences, prepares everything and never does the work. The doers, who never speak to the person. A checker, who is never the doer. The record, which holds every goal, agreement, ruling, rule and result, so that no helper's memory is needed.

**The counterpart is a role, not a long-lived helper.** A helper that runs for weeks, or helpers handing over to each other, lose the thread. So each job the counterpart does is a short run that starts from the record and ends. Nothing needs to stay alive, and there is nothing to hand over. Progress is read from the record: how many of a goal's "done" lines pass their checks.

**Par.** Two cycles for a goal: the agreement and the showing. One more for each decision of taste that needs a sketch first. As trust is earned for a kind of work, par drops to one, then to none: it is done and reported in the weekly ledger. Cycles above par are waste and each one gets a cause.

## 5. What the person sees

Five moments. Plain boxes on purpose: this settles what is on the screen, not how it looks.

**Saying it.** One line, typed or spoken. No form, no choosing of project, helper or machine.

**The agreement.**

```text
YOU SAID     "One place where I can see what needs me."

I HEARD      You want to stop checking many places. One screen, on phone and desk,
             that shows only what needs you, in the order it matters.

DONE MEANS   1. Everything that needs you is on it, and nothing else.
             2. You can answer each thing where it appears.
             3. When nothing needs you, it says so.

I'LL DECIDE  Layout, wording, where it runs.          (point at a line to take it back)

ONLY YOU     When two things need you, which comes first: the oldest, or the one
             holding up the most work?    I would say: the one holding up the most.

NOT DOING    Anything about how the work itself is done.

NEXT         A sketch tomorrow morning. Then silence until it is ready to show.
             Par: 3 cycles (this, the sketch, the showing).

[ Go ]       or point at any line and say what is wrong
```

**The sitting.** When the person chooses to sit down, not when something pings. One thing at a time, in the order of what delay costs.

```text
3 things, about 8 minutes

1 of 3  AGREE    "Faster sign-up": what I heard, what done means            [Go] [Fix a line]

2 of 3  LOOK     "Invoice export" is ready. All 6 "done" lines pass.         [Accept] [Point at what is wrong]
                 I assumed: dates in your time zone; one file a month.

3 of 3  DECIDE   "Price page": yearly price first, or monthly?
                 I would say yearly. If I do not hear by 5 pm, I go with yearly.
                                                                             [Yearly] [Monthly] [Not now…]

That is all. Nothing else needs you. Tomorrow: 2 things expected.
```

**The glance.** Whenever the person wants it. Never pushed.

```text
Nothing needs you.      9 goals moving · 2 showing tomorrow · 1 parked until Monday (your call)

Faster sign-up     being built      shows Thursday      cycle 1, par 2
Invoice export     being checked    shows tomorrow      cycle 1, par 2
Price page         parked           back Monday         you said "not now"

                                                        ▸ Engine room: who is working where
```

**The ledger.** Once a week. It is where trust is earned or lost.

```text
This week    7 goals done. Median 2 cycles (par 2). 41 minutes of your time.
             I decided 23 things for you. 2 are worth a look ▸
             I learned 3 rules from your answers ▸
             1 result was sent back: why, and what I changed ▸
             I asked you 9 questions. Last week: 14.
```

## 6. What this design does not have

- A page for each project to read and keep up with.
- A list of questions that grows.
- Status updates.
- More than one place to look.
- Helpers and machines on the front door. They are one level down, in the engine room. This goes against what Chang asked for on 12 Nov, so it is his call.
- Specifications written by the person.
- The person reviewing anything a check can verify.

## 7. Old questions: snooze, or an "answer later" section?

Recommendation: neither a section nor a pile.

1. **No "answer later" section.** A place where unanswered questions collect is the problem itself. Each one stays in the person's head, and nothing brings it back.
2. **Every question carries its default and a time.** If the choice can be undone, the work goes ahead on the recommendation at that time and the person can overturn it at the showing. Most questions never wait.
3. **"Not now" is a real answer, with a return.** It parks the goal, not the question, until a date or an event the person names: "Monday", "after the release". The record holds it, so the person's memory does not have to. This is the snooze, and it is good because it closes the loop for now.
4. **Questions expire.** When the work has moved on, the counterpart withdraws the question and says so in the ledger. The person is never asked to tidy up.
5. **A cap on what is on the person.** At most a handful at once (seven is a fair start). Above the cap the counterpart must decide more itself, hold a question for the next sitting, or park a goal. It may not add to the pile.

For the 15 questions that have waited 41 to 48 days: none is shown as it stands. The counterpart checks each. If its goal is dead, it is withdrawn. If the choice can be undone, the work goes ahead on the recommendation. If it truly needs him, it is asked again in the next sitting with today's facts. That should leave three to five, not fifteen.

## 9. Prove the method without waiting

The first version of this section asked for a week of running the method by hand. Chang's challenge was fair: the record of how work went already exists, and time is the costly thing. A week of watching would buy one thing that cannot be had sooner: whether trust and silence hold up over time. That is measured in use, from the first day, not before building.

1. **Mine the record.** Every message he typed, every question put to him, every rule he gave, every goal's path, every stall.
2. **Replay the method against it.** For past goals, write the agreement the method would have produced from his first sentence, blind, and compare with what happened.
3. **Draw it, several ways at once.** Separate designers, far-apart premises, one shared test week, counted the same way.
4. **Show him once:** the concepts, his own goals replayed, the findings, and the few decisions only he can make.
5. **Build the meter into the first thing built,** so cycles, minutes and cost are counted from the first real goal.

The plan, the workers and how options are judged are in `from-zero/plan.md`.

## 10. Existing tools

Nothing is inherited. The method needs four things, and each existing tool gets a role only if it fills one of them well:

- something that holds the record;
- something that runs doers and checkers on the machines;
- something that runs checks with no judgement in them;
- something that shows the person the five moments, the same on a phone and a desk.

Which of today's tools fill these, if any, is decided after the work in section 9. Three facts about today's helpers shape the plumbing and nothing else: their effort is cheap next to his attention, so "try and show" is affordable; they forget, so the record must remember for them; and one of them should not judge its own work.
