# Getting work done through others, from zero

Revision 3, 12 Nov 2026. Revision 1 reasoned the method from the problem. Revisions 2 and 3 correct it against evidence: the record of how Chang's work has actually gone (about 62 GB of transcripts, every review page, every board), what is known outside, and a replay of the method against 36 of his past goals. No tool is named until the last section. The plan and the research behind every number are in `from-zero/`.

**What the evidence changed**

0. **The agreement does not save cycles.** Revision 1 called it "the most valuable minute". Two judges, working apart, replayed 36 past goals: an agreement written blind from his first sentence would have prevented about 2 of 416 returns, and as a sitting of its own it adds one per goal. What he wants is mostly found out by reacting to work, not by being asked first. So the say-back stays, because the checks need its "done" lines, but it no longer waits for him unless the work is costly or cannot be undone. The centre of the method moves to showing something cheap and checked, early.
1. The check before showing is the biggest lever.
2. He is the watch today. Nine in ten failures on record were first noticed by him.
3. Silence must be able to say "I cannot see". A quiet system with a broken watcher looks like success.
4. His silence is not consent. A default acts only inside authority he already gave, and only on the side that can be undone.
5. One acceptance earns no trust. Repeated results that stayed right do, by kind of work, and never for what cannot be undone.
6. The limit on what reaches him is minutes and age, not a count.
7. Three things were missing: a rhythm (when he sits down), a way back (stop, undo, take back), and room for discovery and bad news.

## 1. The problem

One person wants more things to exist than they can make alone. Other hands are plentiful. The person's attention is not.

**How little of one person's attention can it take to turn what they want into a finished, correct result made by others?**

Attention is the scarcest thing but not the only limit. The answer must also be correct, do no harm that cannot be undone, and cost a bearable amount of helper effort. Minutes spent recovering from a failure count as attention too.

## 2. Where the attention goes

When work passes through another mind, three gaps open.

| Gap | The question in his head | What the record shows |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Do they know what I want? | He corrected a misunderstanding in 64 typed messages, at about 100 words each. On review pages he typed words on 37% of answers, 38,900 words in all, mostly adding what the options missed. |
| Sight | What is happening? | About 100 typed messages were status questions or nudges. In 78 of 87 failures on record he was the first to notice. |
| Trust | Is it right? | He sent work back for quality in 201 typed messages, more often than he asked for something new (188). Fourteen results were called finished that were not; he caught every one. |

Three more costs come from being one mind: every unfinished thing he must remember; every interruption; and **being the watch**, the standing duty to notice what has stopped.

A perfect helper would need one sentence and return a finished result. Today a goal comes back to him a median of 3 to 9 times, depending on the project, and up to 55.

## 3. Principles

| | Principle | What follows | What the evidence says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spend effort to save attention, within the limits of correctness, harm and cost.** | Try and show, rather than ask. Prepare every decision fully. Count recovery time as attention. | Less of his attention means more work for the staff role. That is a cost in helper effort, and it is real. |
| 2 | **People find out what they want by reacting. Show something cheap and checked, early.** | The first showing comes as soon as there is anything to react to: a sketch, a first slice. For a choice about behaviour, show two concrete alternatives. Gather the decisions he must make into the showing where he can see what they are about. | The replay: about half of all returns were decisions and discoveries that only existed once there was work to look at. No amount of asking up front reached them. |
| 3 | **Say the goal back, and start.** | The say-back goes to him as the work starts: what I heard, what done means, what I will decide. He can fix a line at any time. It waits for his go only when the work is costly, risky or cannot be undone, or when the say-back itself shows real doubt. A one-step request needs none. | The replay: as a gate it prevented about 2 of 416 returns and cost one per goal. It is kept because its "done" lines are the checklist the checks need (8 returns were helpers losing a line already stated). One judge found a third as many wrong assumptions when the writer had his rule book; the other found no difference. |
| 4 | **Decide once, for the case; widen with care.** | An answer settles its own case. A wider rule carries its scope, its reason, the mistake it came from and an end date. A preference is not a permission. The rule must reach the helper at the moment of the decision, and its use is recorded. | He has given 1,757 written rules. At least 8 were corrected again after being written. Only 69 are his own preferences; 1,605 are procedures for particular tools and projects. |
| 5 | **Silence means all is well, and says so honestly.** | No status. One glance shows the truth and how fresh it is. "I cannot see the work" is a state. The watch checks progress on the result, not a pulse, and something watches the watch. | Helpers sent "alive" for hours while producing nothing. One stop raised 472 alarms and restored nothing. In one intensive-care study nine in ten alarms were false; people stop listening. |
| 6 | **Nothing waits on him silently, and nothing waits for ever.** | Every wait has an owner, a consequence and a return time. A default acts only inside authority he already gave and only on the side that can be undone; otherwise the work holds. This covers finished results as well as questions. | A quarter of his answers on review pages clearly did not need him. But defaults pull people toward them, so taking the suggestion 78% of the time does not prove he agreed. |
| 7 | **Check it before he sees it, by someone other than the maker. Then show the thing itself.** | Every "done" line is tied to a check. The proof names the exact result. What was not checked is listed. Approving how it looks is separate from proof that it works. He corrects by pointing at the thing. | The biggest lever in his record: a third of his avoidable typed messages. A second checker does not guarantee quality; the check must be able to fail. |
| 8 | **One counterpart, one doorway, and a way round it.** | One place to say what he wants and to see what needs him. He can always open the original evidence and the checker's own words, and reach stop and undo, without going through the counterpart. | A single doorway can hide the one thing that mattered. Private offices keep the original paper with every summary. |
| 9 | **Trust is earned for each kind of work, and can be lost.** | Results that stayed right, several times, plus his explicit grant, reduce what he is shown. Checks never reduce. A random sample is still shown. One miss, or a change in the work, resets it. Never for money, messages to real people, public releases or anything destroyed. | Quality control and medicine work this way. His work sorts into 12 kinds; test repairs and settled wording could earn trust first. |
| 10 | **Admit work at the rate he can finish it.** | The limit is his minutes and the age of the oldest thing waiting, for results to be shown as well as for questions. Above it, work is held and he is told what is held. | At the peak, 177 goals were open at once. Nobody in the products studied limits work by the person's capacity to review it. |
| 11 | **There is always a way back.** | Stop, change the agreement, take back a permission, and undo are one step away. An accepted result can be reopened, and what was built on it is traced. Before anything that cannot be undone there is a pause: the exact thing, to whom, how much, said back and confirmed. | The record shows results accepted, then found wrong. No design studied follows a bad result through everything built on it. |
| 12 | **Work has a rhythm he sets.** | He sits down at times he chooses. Defaults and "cannot wait" are defined against the next sitting. When he is away, undoable defaults go ahead, everything else holds, and nothing new that needs him starts. | All four designers found the method could not be drawn without this. |
| 13 | **Discovery and bad news are welcome.** | An extra cycle that taught something is not waste and is counted apart from a miss. A checker's doubt reaches him in the checker's words. | Counting every cycle as failure would teach helpers to hide problems. |

## 4. The method

One loop for each goal.

```text
say it ──► say-back ──► silence ──► showing ──► done
 you       (no wait)      │           you: accept, or point at what is wrong
                          └─ a question reaches you only if only you can decide it
```

| Step | What happens | His part |
|---|---|---|
| Say it | A goal in any form, typed or spoken, from anywhere. | Seconds |
| Say-back | What I heard. What done means, as lines that can be checked. What I will decide. When you will first see something. The work starts at once. | Nothing, unless a line is wrong. It waits for his go only when the work is costly, risky or cannot be undone. |
| Silence | The work is done and checked. Forks are settled by earlier rulings or by a default inside authority he gave. What only he can decide is gathered for the showing, where he can see what it is about. | Nothing |
| Showing | The thing itself, as early and as cheap as it can honestly be shown. Each "done" line with its proof. What was not checked. What I assumed. The decisions that are his. | Accept, or point at what is wrong. A correction settles this case; "always?" is asked once. |
| Done | The outcome and any ruling go into the record. | Nothing |

**Starting on the say-back is a permission.** It is his to give and to take back, for everything or for one project, and it is listed with the rest of what he has allowed. Until he has had a first showing, work on a goal is kept to what is cheap to redo, so a wrong line he never read cannot run for days.

**Told, not asked.** A result with nothing left for him to judge, that can be undone, and that is inside what he allowed, is not put to him for acceptance. It is told in one line, with See and Undo. A random one is still shown to him as a real look, so the checks stay honest. In the test week this removed every acceptance of a result that a check had already passed.

**Who does what.** The person. The counterpart, who prepares everything and never does the work. The doers, who never reach him. The checker, who is never the maker, starts from what he asked for and not from the maker's account, and can reach him directly with a doubt. The watch, which is a clock and not a helper. The record, the only memory.

**The counterpart is a role, not a long-lived helper.** Each job is a short run that starts from the record and ends. Nothing stays alive and nothing is handed over. Progress is read from the record: how many "done" lines pass.

**The sitting.** He sits at times he sets. What reaches him is prepared, ordered by what delay costs, and fits his minutes. It ends with "that is all".

**The one interruption.** Something reaches him outside a sitting only if all three hold: only he can do it; it cannot wait for his next sitting; a named loss follows. Each one is counted as a miss, with its cause.

**Par.** One cycle for a piece of work he can accept: the showing. One more for each decision of taste that needs something to look at first. One more when the say-back had to wait for his go. A large goal is cut into slices he can accept, each with its own par. For a kind of work he has granted trust: none, and it is reported in the week's ledger. Days from "said" to "done" are counted beside cycles. Cycles above par each get a cause: a miss, or a discovery.

**What to expect, honestly.** Today a goal comes back a median of 5.5 times. The two replay judges put the best case for the whole method, with every part working, at a little over half of today's returns. The check, the watch and the record account for most of that. The rest are his own decisions and discoveries, which this method can gather and make cheaper but cannot remove. Reaching par on every goal is not promised.

## 5. What he sees

Five moments. Four designers drew them separately, each as a prototype of the same test week; they are in `from-zero/concepts/`. The plain boxes below fix what is on the screen, not how it looks. No word of the method (par, cycle, gate) appears on his screens.

**Saying it.** One line, typed or spoken. No form, no choosing of project, helper or computer.

**The say-back.** It arrives as the work starts. He need not answer it.

```text
YOU SAID     "One place where I can see what needs me."

I HEARD      You want to stop checking many places. One screen, on phone and desk,
             that shows only what needs you, in the order it matters.

DONE MEANS   1. Everything that needs you is on it, and nothing else.
             2. You can answer each thing where it appears.
             3. When nothing needs you, it says so, and says when it last checked.

I'LL DECIDE  Layout, wording, where it runs.            (point at a line to take it back)

YOURS        Which comes first when two things need you: the oldest, or the one holding
             up the most work? I will show you both on the sketch.

FIRST LOOK   A sketch tomorrow morning.

I have started. Point at any line that is wrong.
```

When the work is costly, risky or cannot be undone, the last line reads instead: "This waits for your go, because it cannot be undone."

**The sitting.**

```text
3 things, about 8 minutes

1 of 3  GO?      "Thursday's release" cannot be undone once it is out. Exactly what goes,
                 to whom, and what is not proved.                             [Go] [Not yet]

2 of 3  LOOK     "Invoice export" is ready. All 6 "done" lines pass, checked by someone
                 other than the maker. Not checked: invoices that span two months.
                 I assumed: dates in your time zone; one file a month.        [Accept] [Point at what is wrong]

3 of 3  DECIDE   "Price page": yearly price first, or monthly?
                 I would say yearly. You left layout to me and it can be switched back
                 in a minute, so at 5 pm it goes ahead on yearly.             [Yearly] [Monthly] [Not now…]

That is all. Nothing else needs you. Next sitting: about 5 pm.
```

A choice that cannot be undone, or that he has not given authority for, says instead: "This waits for you. Your silence is not a yes."

**The glance.**

```text
Nothing needs you.   Checked 2 minutes ago.   Next: Invoice export, tomorrow morning.

Faster sign-up       Thursday
Invoice export       tomorrow
Price page           after Monday (you said "not now")
```

Each goal shows only when he will next see it. When the watch cannot see, the glance says "I cannot see the work. Last seen 2:10 pm" and never "Nothing needs you". Who is working where, and what each goal has cost, is one level down.

**The ledger.** Once a week.

```text
This week    7 things done. 41 minutes of your time.
             I decided 23 things for you inside what you allowed. 2 are worth a look ▸
             I learned 3 rules from your answers ▸   Take one back ▸
             2 things came back a second time: one taught us something, one was my miss ▸
             1 thing reached you outside a sitting: why ▸
             I asked you 9 questions. Last week: 14.
```

## 6. What this design does not have

- A page for each project to read and keep up with.
- A list of questions that grows.
- Status updates, heartbeats or alarms that ask nothing of him.
- More than one place to look.
- Helpers and computers on the front door. They are one level down. Chang asked on 12 Nov to see workers per computer on the front page, so this is his call.
- Specifications written by him.
- Him reviewing anything a check can verify.
- Him being the watch.

## 7. Questions that are not answered

Recommendation, unchanged in substance: no "answer later" section.

1. **Every question carries what happens if he does not answer, and when.** If the choice can be undone and is inside authority he gave, the work goes ahead and he can overturn it at the showing. If not, the goal holds and says so.
2. **"Not now" is a real answer.** It parks the goal until a date or an event he names. The record holds it and brings it back.
3. **Questions expire.** When the work has moved on, the counterpart withdraws the question and says so in the ledger. He is never asked to tidy up.
4. **The limit is his minutes and the age of the oldest item.** Above it the counterpart decides more inside its authority, holds a question for the next sitting, or holds a goal.

A correction to revision 1: it said 15 questions had waited 41 to 48 days. The full study counts none as waiting now, because the pages they sat on were retired. That is the real finding: of 3,265 questions ever put to him on review pages, 2,099 were answered and 1,149 simply went away when a page was retired or re-published. Some of those were copies and tests. None was closed with him.

## 9. Proving it without waiting

A week of watching would buy one thing that cannot be had sooner: whether trust and silence hold up over time. That is measured in use, from the first day.

1. **Mine the record.** Done for one computer; the largest computer's transcripts are being read.
2. **Replay the method against it.** Done for 36 past goals: 95 agreements written blind, judged by two separate judges. It overturned the method's first claim (see the top of this file).
3. **Draw it several ways.** Four concepts drawn and walked by four critics. A second round combines the best.
4. **Design the machine several ways.** Four designs written and judged by three critics. They agree on most of it; the combined design is next.
5. **Test on paper before building.** The critics listed cheap trials: kill a helper and see who notices; plant a false "done"; change the amount after approval; forty goals and five days away.
6. **Show him once.**
7. **Build the meter into the first thing built.**

## 10. Existing tools

Nothing is inherited. The method needs four things:

- something that holds the record;
- something that starts short runs of doers and checkers on the computers;
- something that runs checks and clocks with no judgement in them;
- something that shows him the five moments, the same on a phone and a desk, and can reach him with the one interruption.

What each available piece can really do is set out in `from-zero/research/x-what-exists.md`, as facts only. One fact already matters: a short helper run takes about 40 seconds to start, so short runs are affordable but not free. Which pieces are used is decided after the combined design.
